# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
The **26th Shanghai Cooperation Organisation Council of Heads of State** met in Bishkek, Kyrgyz Republic, from 31 August to 1 September 2026, during the SCO's twenty-fifth anniversary year. Leaders adopted the **Bishkek Declaration**, four memoranda of understanding and more than twenty decisions and statements.

India identified thirteen principal outcomes, including an SCO Universal Centre for Countering Security Threats, anti-narcotics cooperation to 2030, and cooperation on energy, disaster management, artificial intelligence, climate, nuclear safety and information. Prime Minister Narendra Modi framed India's approach through **Security, Connectivity and Opportunity**, opposed double standards on terrorism, and said connectivity must respect sovereignty and territorial integrity. India also proposed English as an official language under the SCO Charter and advanced forums for civilisational dialogue, young scientists and young authors.

## 2. Background
The SCO grew from the Shanghai Five, formed in 1996 by China, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Russia and Tajikistan. Uzbekistan joined when the SCO was established at Shanghai on 15 June 2001. Its Charter was signed in 2002 and entered into force in 2003.

India and Pakistan became full members in 2017, Iran in 2023 and Belarus in 2024. The SCO is a Eurasian intergovernmental body, not a military alliance. Its agenda expanded from border confidence-building and the “three evils” of terrorism, separatism and extremism to trade, transport, energy, climate, technology and culture. Its permanent bodies are the Secretariat in Beijing and the Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure, or RATS, in Tashkent.

## 3. Timeline

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Treaty practice**: Summit declarations and MoUs ordinarily express political commitments; domestic legal effect depends on their text and implementation through national law.
- **Article 51**: Directs the State to promote international peace, maintain just relations and foster respect for international law and treaty obligations.
- **Article 253**: Empowers Parliament to legislate for implementing treaties, agreements, conventions and decisions made at international conferences.
- **Domestic safeguards**: SCO security cooperation does not displace the Constitution or Indian rules governing arrest, surveillance and prosecution.
- **Sovereignty and connectivity**: India's position links cross-border projects with respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity.

## 7. CLAT Passage
At a regional summit, leaders adopted a declaration and decisions on terrorism, narcotics, energy, disaster management, artificial intelligence and climate. India proposed Security, Connectivity and Opportunity as three pillars, opposed double standards on terrorism and insisted that connectivity respect sovereignty. It also proposed adding English as an official language under the organisation's Charter.

These are important diplomatic commitments, but they do not automatically rewrite Indian law. Article 51 encourages international peace and respect for international law, while Article 253 enables Parliament to implement treaties and international decisions. Where implementation affects rights, offences or institutions, domestic legal authority remains necessary. The distinction between an international political commitment and an enforceable domestic rule is therefore central.
