# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 1 September 2026, the Supreme Court decided **Ras Al Khaimah Investment Authority v. Matrix Pharmacorp Private Limited**, 2026 INSC 932. The litigation arose from efforts by Ras Al Khaimah Investment Authority (RAKIA) to execute in India a decree obtained in the United Arab Emirates.

The Court upheld the finding that a statement in a counter-affidavit—recording that a company had then decided not to make an acquisition—was not an unconditional undertaking whose breach would support contempt. At the same time, it directed the respondents jointly and severally to provide an additional **₹200 crore security** within two weeks. It left the issues of a unified family-controlled structure and lifting the corporate veil to the executing Commercial Courts at Hyderabad and Ranga Reddy, which were directed to decide the execution proceedings within four months.

## 2. Background
RAKIA is a public entity associated with the Government of Ras Al Khaimah. The dispute concerned funds connected with the VANPIC infrastructure project in Andhra Pradesh and a foreign decree against Nimmagadda Prasad. RAKIA alleged that transactions involving family-controlled companies could frustrate execution.

Section 44A of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 permits a certified decree of a superior court of a notified reciprocating territory to be filed and executed in an Indian district court as if the decree had been passed by that court. The judgment debtor may resist execution on the grounds in Section 13, including want of jurisdiction, absence of a merits decision, fraud, breach of natural justice, refusal to recognise Indian law where applicable, or a claim founded on a breach of Indian law.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Section 44A CPC**: Creates a streamlined execution route for decrees from notified reciprocating territories; it does not make every foreign judgment automatically conclusive.
- **Section 13 CPC**: Lists exceptions to conclusiveness of foreign judgments and remains available in execution.
- **International comity**: Indian courts give appropriate respect to competent foreign courts while retaining statutory safeguards.
- **Contempt undertaking**: A statement becomes an enforceable undertaking only when its words and context create a firm conviction that an undertaking was given. Courts should not invent one by implication.
- **Interim protection**: Security and status-quo orders may preserve assets so that execution does not become illusory, without finally deciding ownership or veil-piercing.
- **Corporate veil**: Separate legal personality is the rule; whether related entities form a unified structure is fact-sensitive and was left to the executing courts.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **CPC, 1908**: Principal procedural statute for civil courts.
- **Foreign judgment**: Section 2(6) defines it as the judgment of a foreign court; Section 2(5) defines a foreign court.
- **Reciprocating territory**: A foreign country or territory notified by the Central Government for Section 44A, with notified superior courts.
- **Execution vs recognition**: Recognition concerns conclusiveness; execution concerns enforcement through court process.
- **Company law**: The corporate veil separates a company from shareholders and related entities, subject to limited exceptions.

## 7. CLAT Passage
A decree-holder obtained a civil decree from a court in a country notified as a reciprocating territory. It filed the decree in India under Section 44A CPC and sought protection against transactions that could place assets beyond execution. A related contempt petition argued that a company had breached a statement recorded in an earlier court order.

The Supreme Court held that contempt could not be founded on an implied undertaking. Words and conduct must clearly show that a binding undertaking was given. Yet this did not require the Court to leave the decree-holder unprotected. It ordered additional security while directing the executing courts to decide whether family-controlled entities formed a unified structure and whether the corporate veil could be lifted. The arrangement preserved assets without prejudging statutory objections under Sections 13 and 44A.

### Questions
1. **Why did the Court reject contempt based on the recorded statement?**:
   - (A) Civil courts cannot record undertakings.
   - (B) The statement did not clearly amount to an unconditional undertaking.
   - (C) Foreign decree-holders cannot seek contempt.
   - (D) Contempt requires a criminal conviction.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: An undertaking must be clear from words and context; it cannot be manufactured by implication.
2. **The additional security order principally served to:**:
   - (A) finally transfer title to all corporate assets
   - (B) punish the respondents for contempt
   - (C) preserve effective execution pending adjudication
   - (D) invalidate the foreign decree
   - *Correct Answer*: C
   - *Explanation*: Security protected the execution process without finally deciding ownership or veil-piercing.
3. **Which provision supplies exceptions to the conclusiveness of foreign judgments?**:
   - (A) Section 9 CPC
   - (B) Section 13 CPC
   - (C) Section 80 CPC
   - (D) Section 151 CPC only
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: Section 13 lists the statutory exceptions.

## 8. AILET MCQs
1. **Execution of a decree from a notified reciprocating territory is principally governed by:**:
   - (A) Section 44A CPC
   - (B) Section 125 CrPC
   - (C) Section 34 IPC
   - (D) Article 352
   - *Correct Answer*: A
   - *Explanation*: Section 44A provides the special execution route.
2. **Which is not an exception listed in Section 13 CPC?**:
   - (A) judgment obtained by fraud
   - (B) proceedings opposed to natural justice
   - (C) judgment not given on merits
   - (D) judgment uses a foreign currency
   - *Correct Answer*: D
   - *Explanation*: Use of foreign currency is not itself a Section 13 exception.
3. **Lifting the corporate veil means:**:
   - (A) treating every shareholder as automatically criminally liable
   - (B) disregarding separate corporate personality in legally justified circumstances
   - (C) converting a company into a partnership
   - (D) removing all limited liability by statute
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: Veil-piercing is an exceptional inquiry into the reality behind separate legal personality.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Recognition vs automatic enforcement**: A foreign decree remains subject to statutory objections.
- **Statement vs undertaking**: A statement of present intention is not necessarily a binding promise to the court.
- **Security vs final liability**: Interim security preserves assets but does not decide ownership.
- **Related companies vs one entity**: Common control alone does not automatically erase separate personality.
- **NCLT/NCLAT vs executing court**: Company-law merger proceedings and decree execution have distinct functions.
