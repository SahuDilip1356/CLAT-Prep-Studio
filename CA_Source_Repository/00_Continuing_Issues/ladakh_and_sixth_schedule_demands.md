# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 20 August 2026, the Union Cabinet approved establishment of a **permanent bench in Ladakh of the High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh**. Ladakh has remained within the jurisdiction of that common High Court since the former State of Jammu and Kashmir was reorganised into two Union Territories in 2019.

The decision is intended to reduce the distance, cost and logistical difficulty faced by litigants from Ladakh. It approves a bench of the existing common High Court; it does not create a separate High Court for Ladakh. The approval must also be distinguished from the continuing political demands for statehood, inclusion under the Sixth Schedule, stronger local lawmaking powers and constitutional safeguards for land, employment, culture and the environment. At the verification cut-off, the Cabinet decision was confirmed, but the bench’s operational notification, place, roster, infrastructure and commencement required separate implementation.

## 2. Background
The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 created the Union Territory of Ladakh without a legislature and the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir with a legislature. Section 75 continued the former State’s High Court as the common High Court for both Union Territories. Litigants from Leh and Kargil have therefore had to access a court whose principal judicial facilities are in Jammu and Srinagar.

Ladakh also has two Autonomous Hill Development Councils established by parliamentary laws for Leh and Kargil. These councils provide decentralised administration but are not Sixth Schedule autonomous district councils. Article 244(2) and the Sixth Schedule currently apply to tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. Extending that model to Ladakh would require constitutional and legislative action; a High Court bench does not accomplish it.

## 3. Timeline
- **1995**: Parliament enacted the Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Council Act for Leh.
- **2003**: A corresponding Autonomous Hill Development Council was established for Kargil.
- **5–6 August 2019**: Parliament enacted the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act and the constitutional arrangement under Article 370 was fundamentally altered.
- **31 October 2019**: The Union Territories of Ladakh and Jammu and Kashmir came into existence; the High Court became their common High Court under Section 75.
- **2020 onward**: Civil society and political groups in Leh and Kargil intensified demands for constitutional safeguards, statehood, legislative representation and Sixth Schedule protection.
- **2024–2026**: The Union government’s High-Powered Committee continued dialogue with Ladakh representatives on safeguards and governance.
- **20 August 2026**: The Union Cabinet approved a permanent bench of the common High Court in Ladakh.
- **Next implementation stage**: Formal orders, judicial allocation, infrastructure and a commencement date must be verified when officially issued.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Union Cabinet**: Approved the permanent High Court bench.
- **Ministry of Law and Justice / Department of Justice**: Handles central administrative work concerning High Courts and benches.
- **High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh**: The common High Court whose permanent Ladakh bench was approved.
- **Chief Justice of the High Court**: Judicial rosters and case allocation remain matters of High Court administration under the applicable legal framework.
- **Lieutenant Governor of Ladakh**: Constitutional-administrative head of the Union Territory under the President.
- **Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils, Leh and Kargil**: Existing statutory local bodies.
- **Leh Apex Body and Kargil Democratic Alliance**: Prominent platforms associated with demands for safeguards and enhanced political representation.
- **Ministry of Home Affairs High-Powered Committee**: Forum for Union government dialogue with Ladakh representatives.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Section 75, J&K Reorganisation Act**: The High Court of Jammu and Kashmir became the common High Court for the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
- **Articles 214 and 231**: Article 214 provides a High Court for each State, while Article 231 permits a common High Court for two or more States or for States and Union Territories. Ladakh’s immediate position also rests on the specific 2019 reorganisation law.
- **Article 230**: Parliament may extend or exclude a High Court’s jurisdiction in relation to a Union Territory.
- **Access to justice**: A local permanent bench can make constitutional and statutory remedies more practically accessible, especially across Ladakh’s vast terrain and harsh travel conditions.
- **Article 239**: Union Territories are administered by the President through an appointed administrator, subject to the Constitution and parliamentary law.
- **Article 244(2) and Sixth Schedule**: The Sixth Schedule establishes autonomous governance for specified tribal areas in four northeastern states. It does not currently include Ladakh.
- **Separate legal questions**: A court bench changes judicial access. Statehood, a territorial legislature, Sixth Schedule status, land safeguards and reservation rules require different constitutional or legislative measures.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **Union Territories created in 2019**: Ladakh, and Jammu and Kashmir.
- **Ladakh districts**: Leh and Kargil are the long-established principal districts; later administrative district announcements do not alter the two Hill Councils’ statutory identity unless implemented by law.
- **Common High Court**: The High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh serves both Union Territories.
- **Sixth Schedule states**: Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.
- **Fifth Schedule**: Applies to administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in states other than the four Sixth Schedule states; it is a different framework.
- **Ladakh geography**: A high-altitude trans-Himalayan region bordering China and Pakistan, containing the Indus valley and the Karakoram and Ladakh ranges.
- **Hill Councils**: Leh and Kargil councils are created by parliamentary statutes, not by the Sixth Schedule.

## 7. CLAT Passage
Parliament’s 2019 reorganisation law created Ladakh as a Union Territory without a legislature and continued the former State High Court as the common High Court for Ladakh and Jammu and Kashmir. In August 2026, the Union Cabinet approved a permanent bench of that common High Court in Ladakh. The decision aims to reduce travel and cost for litigants in a geographically vast, high-altitude region. It does not establish a new High Court, and administrative and judicial steps are still required before the bench begins functioning.

Ladakh’s political groups have separately sought statehood, legislative representation, protection of land and employment, and inclusion under the Sixth Schedule. The Sixth Schedule currently creates autonomous governance arrangements for specified tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. Ladakh’s Leh and Kargil Hill Councils are instead statutory bodies created by parliamentary laws. A local High Court bench improves judicial access, but it neither converts those councils into Sixth Schedule bodies nor gives Ladakh a legislature.

## 11. Geo Card
- **Location**: Ladakh, India’s northernmost Union Territory, with major population centres at Leh and Kargil.
- **Capital**: Leh is the principal administrative headquarters; Kargil is the other major regional centre and Hill Council seat.
- **Strategic significance**: Ladakh borders China and Pakistan, contains sensitive high-altitude frontiers and has long travel distances that make local judicial access especially consequential.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: A separate High Court, Sixth Schedule inclusion, restoration of statehood, creation of a legislature or immediate operational commencement.
- **Why they differ**: The Cabinet approved a bench within the existing common High Court. Each broader governance demand requires its own constitutional, legislative or executive process. Cabinet approval also precedes the formal and practical steps needed to hear cases.
- **Memory clue**: **Bench = access to court; Sixth Schedule = autonomous governance; statehood = constitutional status.**
