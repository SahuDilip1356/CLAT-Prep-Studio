# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 25 July 2026, the Union Law Ministry gave Parliament a consolidated account of the Election Commission of India’s Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. The reply described phased revision across states and Union Territories and detailed inclusion safeguards: repeated Booth Level Officer visits, recorded reasons where enumeration forms are not collected, publication of draft rolls, claims and objections through prescribed forms, two levels of statutory appeal, participation by political parties through Booth Level Agents, and random supervisory audits. It also cited the Supreme Court’s 27 May 2026 judgment upholding the Commission’s power to conduct the exercise under Article 324 and the Representation of the People Act, 1950.

## 2. Background
Electoral rolls determine who may vote in a constituency. Article 324 vests superintendence, direction, and control of elections in the Election Commission, while the Representation of the People Act, 1950 and Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 supply the statutory machinery for preparation, revision, correction, inclusion, and deletion. A Special Intensive Revision is more extensive than a routine summary revision and requires field verification. The current exercise has proceeded in phases, with schedules adjusted in some states. Because erroneous exclusion directly affects electoral participation, notice, reasons, claims, objections, appeals, and transparent draft publication are central safeguards.

## 3. Timeline
- **27 October 2025**: The Election Commission announced a second phase of Special Intensive Revision across multiple states and Union Territories.
- **4 April 2026**: A further phase began in 12 states and Union Territories, including Karnataka, Haryana, and Andhra Pradesh.
- **27 May 2026**: The Supreme Court upheld the Election Commission’s authority to conduct SIR under the 1950 Act and Article 324 in Association for Democratic Reforms v. Election Commission of India.
- **15 July 2026**: The Commission extended revision schedules in Karnataka and certain other states to permit further enumeration and verification.
- **25 July 2026**: The Law Ministry’s parliamentary reply set out the phased exercise, inclusion safeguards, appellate remedies, and audit mechanisms.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Election Commission of India**: Constitutional authority directing electoral-roll preparation and revision.
- **Electoral Registration Officer**: Statutory officer responsible for the constituency roll and first-instance decisions on claims and objections.
- **Booth Level Officer**: Field official who conducts household visits and assists with enumeration and verification.
- **Booth Level Agent**: Political-party representative who participates in the roll-revision process and flags inclusion or deletion issues.
- **District Magistrate or designated appellate authority**: Hears the first statutory appeal, followed by a second appeal to the Chief Electoral Officer.
- **Supreme Court of India**: Reviewed and upheld the Commission’s authority to undertake SIR while emphasising statutory safeguards.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
Article 324 is broad but operates with parliamentary election law. The exercise therefore rests on both constitutional authority and the procedures prescribed by the 1950 Act and 1960 Rules.

- **Article 324 of the Constitution**: vests superintendence, direction, and control of electoral rolls and elections in the Election Commission.
- **Article 326 of the Constitution**: establishes adult suffrage for elections to the House of the People and State Legislative Assemblies, subject to constitutional and statutory qualifications.
- **Representation of the People Act, 1950**: governs electoral rolls, qualification for registration, inclusion, correction, and related appeals.
- **Registration of Electors Rules, 1960**: prescribe forms and procedures for draft publication, claims, objections, corrections, and inclusion.
- **Judicial review**: remains available to test whether constitutional and statutory powers have been exercised lawfully, fairly, and without arbitrary exclusion.

## 6. Static GK Connection
Candidates should distinguish the constitutional body from the field officers and understand that publication of a draft roll is not the end of the process.

- **Election Commission of India**: is a constitutional body headquartered in New Delhi.
- **Electoral roll**: is the official constituency-wise register of eligible electors.
- **Form 6**: is used for new inclusion in the electoral roll.
- **Form 7**: is used to object to a proposed inclusion or seek deletion of an existing entry.
- **Form 8**: is used for correction, shifting of residence within a constituency, replacement EPIC, or marking a person with disability.
- **EPIC**: means Elector Photo Identity Card and is evidence of registration, not the source of the right to be registered.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The Election Commission’s Special Intensive Revision uses house-to-house verification to update electoral rolls. A parliamentary reply dated 25 July 2026 said Booth Level Officers must make two or three visits where needed and record reasons if an enumeration form cannot be collected. Draft rolls are then published, after which electors and political parties may file claims and objections using Forms 6, 7, and 8. Decisions of the Electoral Registration Officer may be appealed first to the District Magistrate or another designated authority and then to the Chief Electoral Officer. Booth Level Agents appointed by political parties may participate, and supervisors conduct random audits. The Supreme Court has upheld the Commission’s authority to undertake SIR under Article 324 and the Representation of the People Act, 1950. This authority does not erase statutory procedure: field verification, notice, reasons, draft publication, remedies, and review help protect eligible electors from wrongful exclusion.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **Summary**: The 25 July parliamentary reply explains how SIR combines ECI’s Article 324 authority with field verification, draft publication, prescribed forms, two appeals, party participation, and audits.
- **Traps**: Article 324 does not place electoral-roll revision outside the 1950 Act and 1960 Rules.
- **Traps**: A draft electoral roll is provisional and remains open to claims, objections, corrections, and appeals.
• Constitutional base: Article 324; adult suffrage link: Article 326.
• Statutory base: Representation of the People Act, 1950.
• Procedure: Registration of Electors Rules, 1960.
• Forms: 6 inclusion, 7 objection/deletion, 8 correction or shifting.
• Appeals: designated district authority, then Chief Electoral Officer.
- **Mnemonic**: “324 directs; 1950 enables; 6-7-8 repairs.”

## 11. Geo Card
- **Location**: Phased implementation across participating Indian states and Union Territories
- **Capital**: Election Commission headquarters, New Delhi
- **Strategic significance**: Accurate rolls are the gateway to electoral participation and affect representation in every revised constituency.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: Special Intensive Revision, summary revision, delimitation, and voter-ID issuance.
- **Why they differ**: Revision updates who is enrolled; delimitation changes constituency boundaries; an EPIC records identity but does not itself create eligibility.
- **Memory clue**: “Roll decides the register; delimitation draws the map.”
