# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 28 August 2026, a Russian strike hit a Ukrainian Defence Forces ammunition-storage site at Myla, west of Kyiv. Secondary explosions sent shells, mines, ammunition, and drones into a nearby residential area. By the monitoring cutoff, Ukrainian authorities reported 37 people killed, 42 injured—including four children—and four missing. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy acknowledged that such a storage site should not have been located there and announced a criminal investigation into possible negligent storage.

The UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine began verifying the incident and reminded all parties of their international humanitarian law (IHL) obligations. Attackers must distinguish military objectives from civilians, avoid excessive incidental harm, and take feasible precautions; defenders must, to the extent feasible, avoid locating military objectives within or near densely populated areas. Available facts do not permit a final conclusion about the strike's proportionality or criminality.

## 2. Background
Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, following its 2014 occupation and purported annexation of Crimea and conflict in the Donbas. The UN General Assembly has repeatedly demanded respect for Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity. Russia is a permanent member of the UN Security Council and can veto substantive Council resolutions.

The law of international armed conflict applies to hostilities between Russia and Ukraine. An ammunition depot can be a military objective if it effectively contributes to military action and its destruction offers a definite military advantage. That does not erase protection for surrounding civilians. A defender's possible breach of precautionary duties also does not release the attacker from distinction, proportionality, and feasible precautions in attack.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Distinction**: Parties must distinguish civilians and civilian objects from combatants and military objectives.
- **Military objective**: An ammunition depot may be targetable if it meets the IHL definition; nearby homes and civilians do not lose protection.
- **Proportionality**: Attack is prohibited if expected incidental civilian harm would be excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated. Information reasonably available before attack matters.
- **Precautions in attack**: The attacker must take feasible steps in verification, means and methods, timing, warnings where effective, and cancellation or suspension if necessary.
- **Precautions against effects**: The defender must, to the extent feasible, remove civilians from the vicinity of military objectives and avoid placing such objectives near dense areas.
- **Independent obligations**: A defender's breach does not cancel the attacker's duties. State responsibility and individual criminal responsibility also use different tests.

## 7. CLAT Passage
A Russian strike hit a Ukrainian ammunition-storage site near a residential area, causing secondary detonations and mass civilian casualties. The depot could be a military objective, but that label does not answer every legal question. The attacker must still assess whether expected civilian harm would be excessive relative to the concrete and direct military advantage and must take feasible precautions. The defender must, so far as feasible, avoid placing military objectives near dense civilian populations. Even if the storage decision breached the defender's duty, the attacker's independent obligations remain. Because investigation is incomplete and information available to the attacker is not public, casualty figures alone cannot establish whether the attack was lawful or a war crime.

### Questions
1. **Which conclusion is best supported by the passage?**:
   - (A) Any ammunition depot may be attacked without further analysis.
   - (B) Civilian deaths automatically prove a war crime.
   - (C) A military objective remains subject to proportionality and precaution duties.
   - (D) A defender's breach cancels attacker obligations.
   - *Correct Answer*: C
   - *Explanation*: Targetability is only the first step; proportionality and precautions still apply.
2. **Why can casualty figures alone not settle proportionality?**:
   - (A) Civilian harm is irrelevant in war.
   - (B) The rule compares expected harm and anticipated advantage using pre-attack information.
   - (C) Proportionality applies only after peace treaties.
   - (D) Only defenders must consider civilians.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: Proportionality is an ex ante assessment, though actual effects may inform an investigation.
3. **If the depot was improperly placed near homes, what follows for the attacker?**:
   - (A) All attack rules cease.
   - (B) Independent duties of distinction, proportionality, and precautions remain.
   - (C) Every nearby civilian becomes a combatant.
   - (D) The UN must approve the strike in advance.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: One party's violation does not release the other party from IHL.

## 9. Q-Cards

- **Back**: Expected incidental civilian harm against the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Military objective vs lawful attack**: A valid target does not eliminate proportionality and precautions.
- **Actual vs expected harm**: Casualties are evidence but do not reproduce the commander's ex ante assessment.
- **State responsibility vs war crime**: A State violation does not automatically establish individual criminal liability.
- **NATO partner vs member**: Ukraine cooperates with NATO but is not covered as a member by Article 5.
- **Strategic autonomy vs neutrality**: India's independent policy is not identical to permanent legal neutrality.
