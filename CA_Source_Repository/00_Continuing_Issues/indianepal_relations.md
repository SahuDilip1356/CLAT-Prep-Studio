# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 26 August 2026, a destructive flash flood in northern Nepal prompted a coordinated Indian response. The Union Home Ministry said the Centre was monitoring the situation with the Ministries of External Affairs, Jal Shakti and Defence; the Home Minister spoke with the Chief Ministers of Bihar and Uttar Pradesh; administrations and rescue teams, including the NDRF, were placed on alert in potentially affected Indian border areas; and the Embassy of India in Kathmandu issued emergency contacts for Indian nationals. Independent reporting confirmed downstream alerts in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh and India's readiness to assist Nepal. The cause and casualty totals were still evolving at the verification cutoff, so this dossier records the settled governmental response rather than provisional figures.

## 2. Background
India and Nepal share an open border, extensive people-to-people ties and several transboundary river systems. Rivers rising in Nepal's Himalayan catchments flow into the Gangetic plain, so a sudden upstream flood can create risks in both countries. Bilateral water cooperation includes a three-tier mechanism established in 2008 and the Joint Committee on Inundation and Flood Management. India has also supported river-training and embankment works in Nepal. Disaster response therefore has both a foreign-policy dimension—humanitarian and consular cooperation with Nepal—and a domestic dimension involving the Union, States, the Central Water Commission and disaster-response forces.

## 3. Timeline
- **1950**: India and Nepal signed the Treaty of Peace and Friendship, a foundational instrument in their modern bilateral relationship.
- **2005**: Parliament enacted the Disaster Management Act, providing India's institutional framework for disaster management.
- **2008**: India and Nepal established a three-tier bilateral mechanism for water resources and flood-management issues.
- **2023**: India's MEA annual report recorded the Joint Committee on Inundation and Flood Management as a forum for bilateral flood and inundation discussions.
- **26 August 2026**: The Nepal flash flood triggered downstream alerts and inter-ministerial monitoring in India.
- **26 August 2026**: Bihar and Uttar Pradesh were advised to remain alert; rescue teams and the NDRF were placed on standby in vulnerable border areas.
- **26 August 2026**: The Embassy of India in Kathmandu activated emergency contacts for affected Indian nationals, while India indicated readiness to assist Nepal.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Ministry of Home Affairs**: Coordinated India's domestic monitoring and communicated with Bihar and Uttar Pradesh.
- **Ministry of External Affairs and Embassy of India, Kathmandu**: Handled bilateral coordination and consular assistance for Indian nationals.
- **Central Water Commission**: Monitors river levels and provides hydrological inputs and flood advisories.
- **National Disaster Response Force**: The specialised response force placed on alert for possible downstream effects.
- **Governments of Bihar and Uttar Pradesh**: Responsible for local preparedness, evacuation and support in vulnerable border districts.
- **Government of Nepal**: Leads response within Nepal and is India's partner in bilateral disaster and water cooperation.
- **Joint Committee on Inundation and Flood Management**: Bilateral mechanism addressing flood-management and inundation issues.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
The event illustrates how foreign affairs and domestic disaster governance operate together.

- **Article 51(c) of the Constitution**: Directs the State to foster respect for international law and treaty obligations.
- **Article 253 of the Constitution**: Empowers Parliament to legislate for implementing treaties, agreements and international decisions.
- **Union List Entry 10**: Places foreign affairs and relations with foreign countries within Parliament's domain.
- **Union List Entry 14**: Covers entering into treaties and agreements and implementing them.
- **State List Entries 17 and 18**: Cover water, irrigation, canals and land, subject to constitutional limits including Union power over inter-State rivers.
- **Article 262 and Union List Entry 56**: Concern adjudication and regulation of inter-State rivers within India; they are not, by themselves, a treaty framework for a river shared with Nepal.
- **Disaster Management Act, 2005**: Establishes national, state and district disaster-management authorities and provides for coordinated response measures.
- **Consular protection**: The Embassy's assistance facilitates communication and evacuation but does not displace Nepal's territorial jurisdiction.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **Nepal**: A landlocked Himalayan country; its capital is Kathmandu.
- **Open border**: Indian and Nepali citizens have a distinctive mobility relationship shaped by bilateral practice and the 1950 treaty.
- **Gandak system**: The Gandak, known upstream as the Narayani, carries Himalayan waters from Nepal into Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.
- **Kosi system**: Another major transboundary river system linking Nepal's catchments with Bihar.
- **NDRF**: A specialised force constituted under the Disaster Management Act, 2005.
- **Central Water Commission**: A technical organisation under the Union Ministry of Jal Shakti dealing with water resources, including flood forecasting.
- **Non-refoulement is unrelated here**: Consular evacuation after a disaster must not be confused with refugee-law protection against forced return.

## 7. CLAT Passage
A flash flood in northern Nepal on 26 August 2026 created an immediate humanitarian emergency and a possible downstream risk for India. The Union Home Ministry said that officials from the Home, External Affairs, Jal Shakti and Defence Ministries were monitoring the event. Bihar and Uttar Pradesh were advised to remain alert, while local administrations, rescue teams and the National Disaster Response Force were placed on standby in border areas that could be affected. The Embassy of India in Kathmandu issued emergency contacts for Indian nationals. These actions illustrate two connected but distinct responsibilities. Nepal retains territorial authority over rescue operations within Nepal, while India may offer humanitarian assistance and provide consular support to its citizens. Inside India, disaster management requires coordination between Union institutions and State and district authorities. Hydrological information is especially important because Himalayan rivers cross political boundaries: a flood wave originating upstream may affect communities downstream without respecting national or state borders. The legal bases must also be kept distinct. Articles 253 and Union List Entries 10 and 14 concern foreign relations and implementation of international agreements. The Disaster Management Act supplies a domestic institutional framework. Article 262 and Union List Entry 56 concern inter-State river regulation within India and do not automatically govern a river dispute with a foreign country.

### Questions
1. **Which inference best follows from the passage?**:
   - (A) India may conduct any operation inside Nepal without Nepal's consent.
   - (B) A cross-border disaster can require simultaneous foreign-policy, consular and domestic preparedness measures.
   - (C) State governments have no role once the NDRF is alerted.
   - (D) Article 262 converts every transboundary river into an inter-State river.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: The passage separates but connects assistance to Nepal, consular support and preparedness within Indian States.
2. **Why does the passage distinguish Article 262 from Articles 253 and Union List Entry 14?**:
   - (A) Article 262 concerns inter-State river disputes within India, while Article 253 and Entry 14 relate to international agreements.
   - (B) Article 262 authorises embassies to evacuate tourists.
   - (C) Article 253 abolishes State disaster authorities.
   - (D) Entry 14 deals only with municipal taxation.
   - *Correct Answer*: A
   - *Explanation*: The legal character of an international river issue differs from an inter-State river dispute within India.
3. **Which action is most directly an exercise of consular assistance?**:
   - (A) The Embassy issuing emergency contacts to Indian nationals in Nepal.
   - (B) Bihar monitoring the Gandak.
   - (C) The Central Water Commission studying river levels.
   - (D) A district administration preparing shelters inside India.
   - *Correct Answer*: A
   - *Explanation*: Helping nationals abroad communicate with their mission is a core consular function.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: Inter-State river disputes inside India versus transboundary river cooperation with Nepal.
- **Why they differ**: Inter-State disputes involve Indian federal units and domestic statutes; transboundary cooperation also engages foreign affairs, treaty practice and Nepal's sovereignty.
- **Memory clue**: “Inter-State is domestic; India–Nepal is international plus domestic preparedness.”
