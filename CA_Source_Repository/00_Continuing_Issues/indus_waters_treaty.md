# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 31 August 2026, the Court of Arbitration in the Indus Waters Western Rivers Arbitration issued an Award on Treaty Status and an order on Pakistan's request for interim measures concerning India's Ratle hydroelectric project. The Court unanimously held, as a ruling of that tribunal, that India's April 2025 decision to place the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance did not suspend or terminate the Treaty or the arbitration. Pending the Neutral Expert's technical decision, expected in July 2027, it directed India not to place concrete above specified levels at the Ratle dam wall and power intake until 90 days after that decision, and required periodic reporting. It declined two other requested measures.

India did not participate in the arbitration and rejected both decisions. The Ministry of External Affairs called the Court illegally constituted, denied that it had jurisdiction, and maintained that the Treaty remains in abeyance. These are opposing legal positions: this dossier records the tribunal's holdings and India's objection without treating either as an agreed settlement or claiming that the Ratle design dispute has been finally decided.

## 2. Background
India and Pakistan signed the Indus Waters Treaty at Karachi on 19 September 1960, with the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (World Bank) also signing for specified purposes. Broadly, the Eastern Rivers—Ravi, Beas and Sutlej—were allocated for India's unrestricted use, while the Western Rivers—Indus, Jhelum and Chenab—were allocated for Pakistan's unrestricted use subject to India's limited domestic, agricultural and hydroelectric uses under the Treaty.

Disagreement over the Kishenganga and Ratle hydroelectric projects produced parallel procedures. Pakistan sought a Court of Arbitration and India sought a Neutral Expert. In October 2022, the World Bank appointed both mechanisms, explaining that the Treaty had assigned it a limited procedural role and that leaving both vacancies unfilled posed practical and legal risks. India has participated in the Neutral Expert process but not in the Court of Arbitration process.

## 3. Timeline
- **19 September 1960**: India and Pakistan signed the Indus Waters Treaty in Karachi.
- **2016**: Pakistan requested a Court of Arbitration over Kishenganga and Ratle; India requested a Neutral Expert for technical questions.
- **17 October 2022**: The World Bank appointed a Neutral Expert and a Chair of the Court of Arbitration under the Treaty.
- **6 January 2025**: The Neutral Expert decided that he was competent to determine the questions referred to him.
- **23 April 2025**: India announced that the Treaty would be held in abeyance following the Pahalgam terrorist attack.
- **8 August 2025**: The Court of Arbitration issued an award on general interpretation of the Treaty.
- **15 May 2026**: The Court issued an award addressing pondage-related issues.
- **31 August 2026**: The Court issued its Award on Treaty Status and limited interim measures concerning Ratle; India rejected both decisions.
- **July 2027, expected**: The Neutral Expert is expected to issue the final decision in his parallel technical proceeding.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **India and Pakistan**: The two State parties to the Treaty and the disputing parties whose legal positions diverge on the Court's jurisdiction and the effect of abeyance.
- **Court of Arbitration**: The tribunal constituted under Annexure G; it issued the 31 August decisions. The Permanent Court of Arbitration acts as its secretariat and is not itself the deciding court.
- **Neutral Expert Michel Lino**: Heads the separate Annexure F process concerning technical questions about Kishenganga and Ratle.
- **World Bank/IBRD**: A signatory for specified Treaty functions; it made the 2022 appointments but does not decide the merits.
- **Ministry of External Affairs, India**: Articulated India's rejection of the arbitral process and decisions.
- **Permanent Indus Commission**: The bilateral institution established by Article VIII to facilitate cooperation and handle questions arising under the Treaty.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Article IX of the Treaty**: Creates a graded structure for resolving questions, differences and disputes through the Permanent Indus Commission, a Neutral Expert or a Court of Arbitration.
- **Annexure F**: Governs referral of specified technical differences to a Neutral Expert.
- **Annexure G**: Governs a Court of Arbitration and authorises interim measures in the circumstances stated there.
- **Parallel proceedings**: The Court and Neutral Expert are distinct mechanisms. The interim order expressly leaves the Neutral Expert to decide the technical merits and is time-limited around that decision.
- **Jurisdictional objection**: A tribunal may rule on its own competence, but a State may reject that competence. The Court's ruling that the Treaty and arbitration continued is therefore a legal holding of that Court, not an agreed India-Pakistan position.
- **Interim versus final relief**: A temporary restraint preserves the position pending adjudication; it does not decide the entire dispute or permanently prohibit the project.
- **Indian constitutional frame**: International treaties are made in the Union's executive domain, while Parliament may legislate under Article 253 to implement treaties. The dispute itself arises primarily under international law and the Treaty, not under Article 262, which concerns inter-State river disputes within India.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **Six rivers**: Eastern—Ravi, Beas, Sutlej; Western—Indus, Jhelum, Chenab.
- **Ratle project**: A run-of-river hydroelectric project on the Chenab in Kishtwar district, Jammu and Kashmir.
- **Kishenganga project**: Located in the Jhelum river system in Jammu and Kashmir.
- **Indus source and course**: The Indus rises on the Tibetan Plateau, flows through Ladakh and Pakistan, and reaches the Arabian Sea.
- **1960 signatories**: Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru, President Mohammad Ayub Khan and the World Bank's representative signed the Treaty.
- **PCA distinction**: The Permanent Court of Arbitration, based in The Hague, supplies administrative support to many tribunals; it is not automatically the tribunal deciding every case it administers.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The Indus Waters Treaty divides the use of the Indus river system between India and Pakistan and establishes several dispute-settlement routes. In a dispute over the Kishenganga and Ratle projects, Pakistan requested a Court of Arbitration while India requested a Neutral Expert. The World Bank filled both appointments in 2022. India participates in the Neutral Expert process but says the Court of Arbitration was illegally constituted.

On 31 August 2026, the Court held that India's decision to place the Treaty in abeyance did not suspend the Treaty or deprive the Court of jurisdiction. It also imposed limited interim measures at Ratle: concrete could not be placed above specified levels at the dam wall and power intake until 90 days after the Neutral Expert's final decision, expected in July 2027. The Court declined two further measures. India rejected the decisions and maintained its contrary jurisdictional and Treaty-abeyance position. The order therefore did not resolve the parties' disagreement about legal authority, decide every Ratle design question, or permanently stop construction. It sought to preserve conditions while the separately appointed Neutral Expert determines technical issues.

### Questions
1. **Which statement is best supported by the passage?**:
   - (A) India accepted the Court's jurisdiction after the interim order.
   - (B) The Court permanently prohibited all work on the Ratle project.
   - (C) The Court issued limited temporary measures while India maintained that the Court lacked jurisdiction.
   - (D) The World Bank itself decided the technical merits.
   - *Correct Answer*: C
   - *Explanation*: The passage describes a limited interim order and expressly preserves India's contrary jurisdictional position.
2. **Why is the expected Neutral Expert decision legally relevant to the interim order?**:
   - (A) The order expires by reference to that decision and leaves technical merits to the separate process.
   - (B) It automatically converts the Neutral Expert into the Court's appellate body.
   - (C) It terminates the Treaty on publication.
   - (D) It permits the World Bank to amend the Treaty.
   - *Correct Answer*: A
   - *Explanation*: The restraint lasts until 90 days after the expected decision and does not displace that technical process.
3. **Which inference should not be drawn from the Court's Award on Treaty Status?**:
   - (A) The Court considered India's abeyance decision insufficient to end its proceeding.
   - (B) India and Pakistan now agree on the Court's jurisdiction.
   - (C) The Treaty contains more than one dispute-settlement route.
   - (D) The parties continue to advance different legal positions.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: India rejected the Court's authority, so no common jurisdictional position exists.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **Trigger**: On 31 August 2026, the Court held that India's abeyance decision did not end the Treaty or arbitration and imposed narrow, time-bound Ratle construction restraints. India rejected the Court and both decisions.
- **Framework**: Article IX plus Annexure F (Neutral Expert) and Annexure G (Court of Arbitration); World Bank's role is limited and procedural.
- **River map**: Eastern—Ravi, Beas, Sutlej; Western—Indus, Jhelum, Chenab; Ratle is on the Chenab in Kishtwar.
- **Core distinction**: Court holding ≠ agreed position; interim measure ≠ final merits; PCA secretariat ≠ deciding tribunal; international dispute ≠ domestic Article 262 dispute.
- **Next marker**: The Neutral Expert's decision is expected in July 2027; the interim restraint extends to 90 days afterward.
- **Mnemonic**: “F finds facts, G gives arbitration; IX routes the dispute.”

## 11. Geo Card
- **Treaty basin**: The Indus system spans the Tibetan Plateau, India, Pakistan and Afghanistan, with its principal lower course and delta in Pakistan.
- **Ratle**: Chenab River, Kishtwar district, Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir, India.
- **Kishenganga**: Jhelum system in the Kashmir region.
- **Institutional location**: The PCA is headquartered at the Peace Palace in The Hague, Netherlands.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Tribunal view vs India's view**: State both; do not present the 31 August ruling as mutually accepted.
- **Interim order vs merits award**: The Ratle restraint is temporary and does not decide all technical design questions.
- **PCA vs Court of Arbitration**: The PCA provides secretariat services; the case-specific tribunal issued the decisions.
- **Neutral Expert vs arbitrator**: The Neutral Expert is a separate Treaty mechanism, not an appeal from the Court.
- **Eastern vs Western Rivers**: Ravi, Beas and Sutlej are Eastern; Indus, Jhelum and Chenab are Western.
- **Article 262 vs Article 253**: Article 262 concerns domestic inter-State water disputes; Article 253 concerns implementing international obligations.
