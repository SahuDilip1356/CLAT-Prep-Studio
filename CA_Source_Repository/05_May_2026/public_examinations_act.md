# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 24 July 2026, the Prime Minister’s Office said the Union Cabinet had approved stricter action against examination paper leaks. A draft Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 was subsequently reported as having been circulated to Members of Parliament. The proposal would increase punishment for individual offenders and organised crime, create a Special Task Force for specified cases, and provide for fast-track trial by designated Sessions Courts. These are proposed changes: until Parliament passes the Bill and it receives presidential assent and commencement as required, the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 remains the governing central law in its existing form.

## 2. Background
The 2024 Act was enacted as Act 1 of 2024 and came into force on 21 June 2024. It targets unfair means in public examinations conducted by authorities listed in its Schedule, including leakage of question papers, unauthorised assistance, tampering with computer systems, and collusion by service providers. Candidates are not treated as offenders under this central Act and remain governed by the rules of the relevant examination authority. The current statute distinguishes ordinary offences from organised crime and permits investigation by an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police or Assistant Commissioner of Police. The 2026 draft seeks to strengthen that framework after recurring paper-leak controversies.

## 3. Timeline
- **12 February 2024**: The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 received presidential assent.
- **21 June 2024**: The central government brought the 2024 Act into force.
- **23 July 2026**: The Prime Minister announced that fast-track courts would hear paper-leak cases.
- **24 July 2026**: The PMO stated that the Union Cabinet had approved stricter legal action against paper leaks.
- **25 July 2026**: The Indian Express reported that the draft 2026 amendment had been circulated to MPs with enhanced punishments, a Special Task Force, and time-bound trials.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Union Cabinet**: Approved the proposal for stricter legal action according to the PMO.
- **Parliament of India**: Must consider and pass any amendment before it can alter the 2024 Act.
- **Public examination authorities**: Bodies named in the Act’s Schedule whose examinations receive statutory protection.
- **State governments and Union Territory administrations**: Would designate fast-track Sessions Courts in consultation with the Chief Justice of the relevant High Court under the reported draft.
- **Special Task Force**: The proposed investigating unit for specified organised paper-leak cases, with a reported two-month investigation target.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
The development tests accurate legislative-status reading. Cabinet approval and circulation of a draft do not themselves amend an Act; the proposal must complete the parliamentary and constitutional process.

- **Article 107 of the Constitution**: governs introduction and passage of ordinary Bills in Parliament.
- **Article 111 of the Constitution**: concerns presidential assent to Bills passed by Parliament.
- **Public Examinations Act, 2024**: currently provides three to five years’ imprisonment and a fine up to ₹10 lakh for ordinary offences, with a minimum five years and a fine of at least ₹1 crore for organised crime.
- **Reported 2026 draft**: proposes five to ten years and a fine up to ₹50 lakh for ordinary offenders, and a minimum seven years with a fine of at least ₹10 crore for organised crime.
- **Fast-track Sessions Courts**: remain part of the ordinary judiciary; speed of trial does not displace fair-trial protections or High Court supervision.

## 6. Static GK Connection
This issue connects the stages of legislation with criminal procedure and the institutional structure of public examinations.

- **Ordinary Bill**: may originate in either House of Parliament and generally requires passage by both Houses and presidential assent.
- **Sessions Court**: is the principal criminal court at the district level under the High Court’s judicial supervision.
- **Cognisable offence**: permits police arrest without warrant, subject to statutory safeguards.
- **Non-bailable offence**: does not mean bail is impossible; it means bail is not available as an automatic right and depends on judicial discretion.
- **Scheduled authority**: the central Act applies to examinations conducted by authorities specified in its Schedule, rather than every examination held in India.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 criminalises paper leakage, unauthorised assistance, tampering, and organised cheating in public examinations conducted by scheduled authorities. The Act presently provides three to five years’ imprisonment and a fine up to ₹10 lakh for ordinary offences. Organised crime attracts at least five years’ imprisonment and a fine of at least ₹1 crore. In July 2026, the Union Cabinet approved a proposal to strengthen the law. According to the draft reported as circulated to MPs, ordinary punishment would rise to five to ten years with a fine up to ₹50 lakh, while organised crime would attract at least seven years and a fine of at least ₹10 crore. The proposal also contemplates a Special Task Force, a two-month investigation target, designated Sessions Courts, trial within three months after filing of the charge sheet, and transfer of pending cases. Cabinet approval does not enact these changes. They become law only after the legislative process, presidential assent, and any required commencement step.

   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: The reported draft provides for a Special Task Force and fast-track trial in designated Sessions Courts.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **Summary**: The Cabinet-approved 2026 draft would sharply enhance anti-paper-leak penalties and create time-bound investigation and trial mechanisms, but the existing 2024 Act remains law until the amendment is enacted.
- **Traps**: Do not describe Cabinet approval or circulation to MPs as enactment.
- **Traps**: “Non-bailable” does not mean a court can never grant bail.
• Parent law: Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: Cabinet approval, introduction of a Bill, parliamentary passage, presidential assent, and statutory commencement.
- **Why they differ**: These are distinct constitutional and legislative stages; a proposal does not alter the statute merely because the Cabinet approved it.
- **Memory clue**: “Cabinet proposes; Parliament passes; President assents; notification may commence.”
