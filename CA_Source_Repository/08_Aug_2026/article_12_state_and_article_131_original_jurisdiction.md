# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
In Lucknow Development Authority v. Union of India, 2026 INSC 923, the Supreme Court held that a statutory authority cannot invoke the Court's original jurisdiction under Article 131. The Allahabad High Court had dismissed a long-pending writ petition by the Lucknow Development Authority (LDA), reasoning that the land dispute involved the Union and a State and should be taken directly to the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court set that order aside and remitted the writ petition to the High Court for an expeditious merits decision.

The Court stressed that LDA may fall within the broad expression “the State” in Article 12 for fundamental-rights scrutiny, but it is not a constituent “State” named in the First Schedule. Article 131 uses the latter, narrower meaning. The judgment was delivered on 21 August 2026 and published on the Supreme Court's latest-judgments page on 29 August 2026.

## 2. Background
LDA is a statutory body constituted under the Uttar Pradesh Urban Planning and Development Act, 1973. Its writ petition concerned land connected with a residential colony and claims involving defence authorities. After the petition remained pending for about twenty-five years, the High Court treated the controversy as one for Article 131 and granted liberty to approach the Supreme Court.

Article 12 defines “the State” for Part III accountability and includes governments, legislatures, local authorities, and “other authorities.” Article 131 creates the Supreme Court's exclusive original jurisdiction over specified disputes among the Government of India and one or more constituent States, provided the dispute involves a question on which a legal right depends. Similar words must be read in their text, purpose, and constitutional setting.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Article 12** is an inclusive definition used for Part III accountability. A statutory body may be an “other authority.”
- **Article 131** is a jurisdiction-conferring provision. Its “States” are constituent States of the Union, not every Article 12 authority.
- Article 131 covers only the party combinations listed in it and disputes involving the existence or extent of a legal right.
- **Exclusive original jurisdiction** means a qualifying dispute begins in the Supreme Court; it does not absorb every dispute involving a governmental instrumentality.
- The Court did **not** decide the underlying land title. It corrected the jurisdictional error and restored the High Court proceeding.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- Article 131 lies in Part V, Chapter IV of the Constitution.
- The First Schedule lists India's States and Union territories.
- Article 32 gives the Supreme Court writ jurisdiction for fundamental rights; Article 226 gives High Courts wider writ jurisdiction for fundamental rights and “any other purpose.”
- Article 136 concerns special leave to appeal and differs from Article 131 original jurisdiction.
- The Supreme Court began functioning on 28 January 1950 and is seated in New Delhi.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The Constitution sometimes uses the same word for different purposes. Article 12 enlarges “the State” so governments, local authorities, and qualifying “other authorities” can be tested against fundamental rights. Article 131 instead identifies a narrow set of federal disputes that may begin directly in the Supreme Court. In Lucknow Development Authority v. Union of India, the Court held that a statutory development authority does not become a constituent State merely because it may be “State” under Article 12. The High Court therefore could not send LDA to Article 131 and avoid deciding its writ petition. The Supreme Court restored the petition without deciding the underlying land rights.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Article 12 vs Article 131**: Broad constitutional accountability is not capacity to initiate a federal suit.
- **Original vs appellate jurisdiction**: An Article 131 suit begins in the Supreme Court; this judgment arose from an appeal.
- **Remand vs final merits ruling**: Restoration of the writ is not a decision on land ownership.
- **State vs Union territory**: Article 131's party text should not be casually expanded.
