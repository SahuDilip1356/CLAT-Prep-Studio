# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 20 August 2026, the Supreme Court delivered **Ramkrishna Chauhan v. State of Uttar Pradesh**, 2026 INSC 895. It quashed proceedings against a school manager under Sections 3(1)(r) and 3(1)(s) of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989.

The alleged incident occurred inside an enclosed school room with no window or public access. The FIR and witness statements did not attribute a specific caste-based utterance to the appellant or establish that a member of the public witnessed or heard it. The Court held that the statutory ingredient “in any place within public view” was not prima facie satisfied. Proceedings for the remaining IPC offences were allowed to continue.

## 2. Background
The SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act was enacted in 1989 to prevent specified atrocities against members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, provide Special Courts and support relief and rehabilitation. Sections 3(1)(r) and 3(1)(s) apply to a person who is not a member of an SC or ST.

Section 3(1)(r) punishes intentional insult or intimidation with intent to humiliate an SC/ST member in any place within public view. Section 3(1)(s) punishes abuse of an SC/ST member by caste name in any place within public view. Earlier Supreme Court decisions, including **Hitesh Verma v. State of Uttarakhand** and **Karuppudayar v. State**, distinguished a place open to public witnessing or hearing from conduct wholly within four walls without public presence.

## 3. Timeline
- **1989**: Parliament enacted the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act.
- **30 January 1990**: The Act came into force.
- **2015–2016**: Parliament’s amendment expanded and reorganised the list of atrocities, including the present clauses (r) and (s).
- **2020**: In Hitesh Verma, the Supreme Court explained the “public view” ingredient in the context of alleged insult inside a building.
- **25 January 2020**: The FIR underlying Ramkrishna Chauhan was registered after a school dispute.
- **10 April 2023**: The Allahabad High Court declined to interfere with the summoning order.
- **20 August 2026**: The Supreme Court quashed only the SC/ST Act proceedings against the appellant while preserving the IPC proceedings.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Essential ingredients**: Even at the prima facie stage, the FIR, chargesheet and supporting material must disclose the basic elements of the alleged statutory offence.
- **“Public view”**: Members of the public must be able to witness or hear the alleged utterance. A location does not qualify merely because it sits within a school or other premises generally associated with public activity.
- **Public place versus public view**: The statutory phrase focuses on visibility or audibility to the public, not simply ownership or classification of the location.
- **Specific allegation**: The Court also noted the absence of any specific caste-based words attributed to the appellant in the FIR or the complainant’s statement.
- **Limited quashing**: The ruling removed the SC/ST Act counts because their ingredients were absent; it did not adjudicate guilt on the surviving IPC allegations.
- **Constitutional setting**: Articles 17, 46, 338 and 338A reflect the constitutional commitment against untouchability and caste oppression. That objective coexists with the criminal-law requirement that every statutory ingredient be disclosed.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **Article 17**: Abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form.
- **Article 46**: Directs the State to promote the educational and economic interests of weaker sections, especially SCs and STs, and protect them from social injustice and exploitation.
- **Article 338**: Provides for the National Commission for Scheduled Castes.
- **Article 338A**: Provides for the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes.
- **Special Courts**: The 1989 Act provides for Exclusive Special Courts or Special Courts for speedy trial.
- **Scheduled Castes and Tribes**: Constitutional specification operates principally through Articles 341 and 342.

## 7. CLAT Passage
Two clauses of a special criminal statute punish intentional caste-based humiliation and abuse by caste name when committed “in any place within public view.” An FIR alleged that a school manager abused and assaulted a parent inside a school office. The site plan placed the incident in an enclosed room without a window or public access. Teachers said that a quarrel occurred on the school premises, but none said they were inside the room or heard a caste-based utterance. The FIR and the complainant’s statement also did not identify specific caste-based words spoken by the manager.

The Supreme Court held that a school is not automatically a place “within public view” for every event occurring inside it. The question is whether members of the public could witness or hear the alleged utterance. Although a court does not meticulously weigh evidence at the cognisance stage, the basic ingredients of the offence must appear in the material. The Court therefore quashed the charges under the special Act, while allowing the independent charges under the general penal code to continue.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **Summary**: In 2026 INSC 895, the Supreme Court held that an alleged utterance inside an enclosed school room was not shown to be “within public view,” so Sections 3(1)(r) and (s) were not prima facie made out.
- **Test**: Could members of the public witness or hear the alleged caste-based insult or abuse?
- **Second defect**: No specific caste-based utterance was attributed to the appellant.
- **Procedure**: At cognisance, courts do not conduct a trial but must find the basic statutory ingredients.
- **Constitution links**: Articles 17, 46, 338, 338A, 341 and 342.
- **Outcome**: Special-law proceedings quashed; IPC proceedings preserved.
- **Traps**: A public institution is not automatically “within public view” in every interior room.
- **Traps**: The judgment did not dilute the Act where all statutory ingredients are properly alleged.
- **Mnemonic**: “Public view asks who could see or hear.”

## 11. Geo Card
- **Location**: The prosecution arose in Uttar Pradesh and was reviewed by the Allahabad High Court before reaching the Supreme Court in New Delhi.
- **Capital**: Lucknow is Uttar Pradesh’s capital; New Delhi is the seat of the Supreme Court.
- **Strategic significance**: Special Courts under the central Act function across states, while appeals and judicial review safeguard both statutory protection and criminal-process requirements.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: “Public place,” proof required after a complete trial, or a ruling that caste abuse is lawful in private.
- **Why they differ**: “Within public view” concerns whether the public could witness or hear the conduct. The case examined whether statutory ingredients appeared prima facie, not whether all private caste abuse is acceptable. Other offences and civil or constitutional consequences can remain.
- **Memory clue**: **Place is not enough; public visibility or audibility matters.**
