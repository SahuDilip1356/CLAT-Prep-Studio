# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 22 August 2026, the National Biodiversity Authority announced the disbursal of **₹2.82 crore** under India's access and benefit-sharing (ABS) mechanism. Of this, **₹2.80 crore** was released to 27 State Biodiversity Boards and three Union Territory Biodiversity Councils. The underlying payment came from Advanta Enterprises Limited, formerly UPL Limited, for use of genetic resources associated with 24 hybrids: eight cauliflower, five hot-pepper, five okra and six tomato hybrids.

Because the specific places from which the biological resources were accessed and the individual benefit claimers could not be identified, the amount was distributed among the State and Union Territory institutions on the basis described by the Authority. A further **₹2.01 lakh** was released to ICAR-Indian Institute of Horticultural Research, Karnataka, in relation to two microbial strains used by Sumitomo Chemical India Limited. The release said total ABS disbursals had crossed **₹185 crore**.

## 2. Background
Access and benefit-sharing seeks to prevent biological resources and associated traditional knowledge from being commercially used without fair returns to the country and communities that provide them. Benefits can be monetary, such as royalties and fees, or non-monetary, such as technology transfer, research participation, training and sharing of research results.

India implements this idea through the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002**, as amended, together with rules and ABS regulations. The Act creates a three-level institutional structure: the National Biodiversity Authority at the Union level, State Biodiversity Boards at the State level and Biodiversity Management Committees at the local-body level. At the international level, ABS is one of the three objectives of the Convention on Biological Diversity and is elaborated by the Nagoya Protocol.

## 3. Timeline
- **1992**: The Convention on Biological Diversity is opened for signature at the Rio Earth Summit.
- **2002**: Parliament enacts the Biological Diversity Act.
- **2003**: The National Biodiversity Authority is established in Chennai.
- **29 October 2010**: The Nagoya Protocol on ABS is adopted in Nagoya, Japan.
- **12 October 2014**: The Nagoya Protocol enters into force internationally.
- **2014**: India issues national ABS guidelines under the Biological Diversity Act.
- **1 April 2024**: The Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 comes into force.
- **2025**: Updated Biological Diversity Rules and ABS Regulations take effect.
- **22 August 2026**: NBA announces the ₹2.82 crore disbursal and reports cumulative releases above ₹185 crore.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)**: Statutory national authority that determines benefit-sharing for approvals within its jurisdiction.
- **State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs)**: State-level institutions involved in regulating and advising on access and use within the statutory framework.
- **Union Territory Biodiversity Councils**: Perform corresponding functions in Union Territories covered by the disbursal.
- **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)**: Local-body committees that document and conserve biodiversity and may represent local benefit claimers.
- **Advanta Enterprises Limited**: Company whose ABS payment related to 24 vegetable hybrids.
- **ICAR-Indian Institute of Horticultural Research**: Institutional recipient connected to the microbial-strain payment.
- **Convention on Biological Diversity Secretariat**: Supports the CBD and Nagoya Protocol framework internationally.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Section 21, Biological Diversity Act**: Requires the NBA, while granting relevant approval, to secure fair and equitable sharing of benefits on mutually agreed terms between the applicant and the BMC represented by the NBA.
- Section 21 recognises multiple benefit forms, including joint intellectual-property ownership, technology transfer, association of Indian scientists and communities, venture support, monetary compensation and non-monetary benefits.
- Money may be directed to the National Biodiversity Fund or, where a resource or knowledge is traced to an individual, group or organisation, paid directly to the benefit claimer according to law and agreement.
- **Federal and local design**: NBA, SBBs and BMCs distribute authority across national, State and local institutions. A People’s Biodiversity Register records local biological resources and knowledge but does not itself transfer ownership of every recorded item.
- **Article 253** allows Parliament to legislate for implementing treaties and international decisions. The 2002 Act gives domestic legal form to biodiversity commitments; the CBD or Nagoya Protocol does not by itself replace the Act's procedures.
- **Articles 48A and 51A(g)** connect State policy and citizens' duties to environmental protection. **Article 21** underpins the wider constitutional jurisprudence on a healthy environment.
- ABS combines conservation with distributive justice: commercial users contribute to those who conserve or provide biological resources and associated knowledge.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- The **Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)** has three objectives: conservation of biodiversity, sustainable use of its components, and fair and equitable sharing of benefits from genetic resources.
- The **Nagoya Protocol** is a supplementary agreement to the CBD, adopted in 2010 and in force since 2014.
- The NBA is headquartered in **Chennai, Tamil Nadu**.
- **Prior informed consent** and **mutually agreed terms** are central international ABS concepts.
- The Nagoya Protocol also covers traditional knowledge associated with genetic resources under its access, benefit-sharing and compliance provisions.
- **Target 13** of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework concerns effective legal, policy and administrative measures for benefit-sharing from genetic resources, digital sequence information and associated traditional knowledge.

## 7. CLAT Passage
A seed company commercially uses genetic resources connected to multiple crop hybrids and makes a benefit-sharing payment to the statutory national authority. If the source communities and collection locations can be identified, the legal framework can direct benefits toward those claimers. If they cannot be identified, the law still permits institutional routes that support conservation, local biodiversity documentation and community-oriented work. This illustrates that ABS is neither a general tax on agriculture nor a private charity. It is a statutory arrangement linking permission to use biological resources with fair returns under agreed terms. The framework operates through national, State and local institutions and also helps India implement the Convention on Biological Diversity and the Nagoya Protocol.

   - (A) The absence of an identified individual automatically cancels every conservation duty.
   - (B) Institutional distribution can still channel the payment toward statutory conservation and community purposes.
   - (C) All genetic resources become the company's unrestricted property.
   - (D) The payment must become customs duty.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: ABS law provides institutional mechanisms so benefits can serve conservation and community purposes even where direct claimers are untraceable.
2. **Which statement best distinguishes ABS from a general tax?**:
   - (A) ABS is tied to access or utilisation of biological resources and agreed benefit-sharing conditions.
   - (B) ABS is imposed on every citizen regardless of biological-resource use.
   - (C) ABS can never include non-monetary benefits.
   - (D) ABS is collected only by municipal property registrars.
   - *Correct Answer*: A
   - *Explanation*: The statutory mechanism connects specified access or utilisation with fair sharing on defined terms.
3. **What role does Article 253 play in this context?**:
   - (A) It makes every treaty clause automatically superior to the Constitution.
   - (B) It empowers Parliament to legislate for implementation of international agreements and decisions.
   - (C) It creates the National Biodiversity Authority directly.
   - (D) It prevents India from joining environmental treaties.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: Article 253 supplies Parliament's treaty-implementation power; domestic rights and duties operate through enacted law.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Biological resource vs every commodity**: The Act defines its scope and contains statutory exemptions; ABS is not an automatic levy on every traded farm product.
- **NBA vs SBB vs BMC**: National approval and benefit-sharing functions, State regulation/advice and local documentation/conservation are distinct roles.
- **CBD vs Nagoya Protocol**: CBD is the parent convention; Nagoya is its supplementary ABS agreement.
- **ABS vs intellectual-property ownership**: Benefit-sharing may include joint IP ownership, but ABS does not automatically assign every patent to a community.
- **Direct claimant vs institutional allocation**: Identifiable claimers may receive direct benefits; untraceable claims can require a lawful fund or institutional route.
- **People’s Biodiversity Register vs title deed**: A register documents resources and knowledge; it is not automatically conclusive private-property title.
