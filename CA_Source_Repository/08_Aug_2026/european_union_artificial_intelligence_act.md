# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 2 August 2026, the main application milestone under the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act arrived. Transparency duties under Article 50 became applicable, including duties to tell people when they are interacting directly with certain AI systems and to make AI-generated or manipulated content identifiable in specified circumstances. The European Commission's AI Office also obtained enforcement powers over general-purpose AI models. This did not make every high-risk-system obligation immediately applicable: a 2026 amending measure postponed major high-risk rules to later dates.

## 2. Background
The AI Act is Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, adopted in 2024 as a comprehensive, risk-based framework for artificial intelligence. It applies different duties according to the risk posed by an AI use. Some practices are prohibited; high-risk systems face controls concerning risk management, data, documentation, human oversight, accuracy, cybersecurity, and fundamental-rights impacts; certain AI systems face transparency duties; and general-purpose AI models have a separate regulatory layer. The Regulation can apply to providers outside the EU when they place covered systems or models on the EU market, or where the output produced by the system is used in the EU. Its obligations have been phased in since 2025 and will continue to phase in through 2028.

## 3. Timeline
- **13 June 2024**: The European Parliament and Council adopted Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Artificial Intelligence Act.
- **1 August 2024**: The Regulation entered into force, twenty days after publication in the Official Journal.
- **2 February 2025**: Rules on prohibited AI practices, definitions, and AI literacy began to apply.
- **2 August 2025**: Governance provisions and obligations for general-purpose AI models began to apply.
- **27 July 2026**: The Digital Omnibus amending Regulation entered into force and revised the high-risk implementation timetable.
- **2 August 2026**: Article 50 transparency duties became applicable and the AI Office obtained enforcement powers concerning general-purpose AI models.
- **2 December 2027**: The revised date for major obligations concerning high-risk systems listed in Annex III.
- **2 August 2028**: The revised date for high-risk systems embedded in regulated products listed in Annex I.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **European Parliament and Council of the European Union**: The EU co-legislators that adopted the AI Act.
- **European Commission**: The EU executive institution responsible for implementation measures and guidance.
- **European AI Office**: The Commission body responsible for EU-level supervision and enforcement concerning general-purpose AI models and certain other tasks.
- **National competent authorities**: Member-state bodies responsible for supervision and enforcement concerning AI systems within their assigned jurisdiction.
- **European Artificial Intelligence Board**: A body supporting consistent application and cooperation across the EU.
- **Providers and deployers**: The Act places different obligations on those that develop or place AI systems on the market and those that use them under their authority.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
The AI Act is a binding EU regulation with direct applicability in member states. It creates a common market rule while seeking a high level of protection for health, safety, and fundamental rights.

- **Risk-based regulation**: Legal duties intensify with the potential harm of an AI use, ranging from prohibited practices to high-risk controls and transparency requirements.
- **Fundamental-rights protection**: The framework addresses equality, privacy, data protection, dignity, freedom of expression, effective remedy, and democratic integrity in contexts such as biometric identification, employment, education, policing, and access to essential services.
- **Article 50 transparency**: Covered providers must inform people when they interact directly with AI, while specified AI-generated or manipulated content must be marked or disclosed; deployers also have duties for deepfakes and certain public-interest content.
- **Extraterritorial reach**: A provider established outside the EU can still fall within scope when it places covered technology on the EU market or when the system's output is used in the EU.
- **Enforcement and proportionality**: National authorities and the AI Office divide enforcement responsibility. The Act provides tiered administrative fines, with the highest statutory ceiling for certain prohibited practices reaching the greater of €35 million or 7 per cent of worldwide annual turnover, subject to the applicable legal conditions.

## 6. Static GK Connection
The issue links EU institutions, types of EU law, digital regulation, and human-rights governance.

- **EU regulation versus directive**: A regulation is binding in its entirety and directly applicable; a directive binds member states as to the result but normally requires national implementation.
- **EU membership**: The European Union has 27 member states and is distinct from the 46-member Council of Europe.
- **General-purpose AI model**: A model capable of competently performing a wide range of distinct tasks, which may be integrated into many downstream systems.
- **Deepfake**: AI-generated or manipulated image, audio, or video content resembling real persons, objects, places, entities, or events and appearing authentic or truthful.
- **Risk tiers**: Prohibited practices, high-risk systems, transparency-risk uses, and lower-risk uses do not carry identical legal duties.
- **Brussels effect**: Large-market regulation may influence business conduct and technical standards beyond the regulator's territory.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act is a regulation built around differentiated risk. It prohibits certain practices, places extensive controls on high-risk systems, regulates general-purpose AI models, and imposes transparency duties on specified uses. The Regulation entered into force on 1 August 2024, but its obligations were deliberately phased. Rules on prohibited practices began to apply in February 2025, while governance and general-purpose-model duties followed in August 2025. On 2 August 2026, Article 50 transparency duties became applicable. These include informing people when they interact directly with certain AI systems and identifying specified AI-generated or manipulated content. Deployers must also disclose deepfakes and some AI-generated public-interest content. The AI Office simultaneously obtained enforcement powers concerning general-purpose AI models. However, a 2026 amendment postponed major obligations for Annex III high-risk systems until December 2027 and for high-risk systems embedded in regulated products until August 2028. The Act may reach providers outside the EU when they place covered technology on the EU market or when output produced by the system is used in the EU. Thus, the 2026 milestone combines transparency, market access, institutional enforcement, and fundamental-rights protection without making every provision applicable on the same day.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **Summary**: The EU AI Act is a directly applicable, risk-based Regulation whose Article 50 transparency duties and AI Office enforcement milestone arrived on 2 August 2026, while major high-risk-system obligations remain scheduled for 2027 and 2028.
- **Traps**: Do not say the AI Act entered into force on 2 August 2026; it entered into force on 1 August 2024 and applies through a phased timetable.
- **Traps**: Do not equate the European Union with the Council of Europe, or assume that a regulation and a directive operate identically.
• Instrument: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.
• Regulatory method: differentiated obligations based on risk.
• 2026 milestone: Article 50 transparency and AI Office enforcement powers.
• Reach: covered providers or uses may fall within scope even outside the EU.
• High-risk timetable: Annex III on 2 December 2027; product-linked Annex I systems on 2 August 2028.
- **Mnemonic**: “24 force, 26 disclose, 27–28 high risk”—entry into force in 2024, transparency in 2026, and revised high-risk dates in 2027 and 2028.

## 11. Geo Card
- **Location**: European Union, a political and economic union of 27 European member states
- **Capital**: The EU has no formal capital; Brussels hosts the European Commission and Council and is a principal institutional centre.
- **Strategic significance**: Access to the EU single market gives its digital rules potential influence over companies, products, and technical practices beyond Europe.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: Entry into force on 1 August 2024 versus the main application milestone on 2 August 2026.
- **Why they differ**: Entry into force made the Regulation part of EU law, while the phased application provisions specify when particular duties and enforcement powers become operational.
- **Memory clue**: “In force first; duties unfold later.”
