# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 20 August 2026, the Supreme Court delivered its judgment in **Shiv Pratap Singh alias Chinu v. State of Uttar Pradesh**, 2026 INSC 894. It quashed proceedings initiated against the appellants under Sections 2 and 3 of the Uttar Pradesh Gangsters and Anti-Social Activities (Prevention) Act, 1986.

The Court held that the Act defines a “gang” and a “gangster” but does not create a distinct substantive offence whose commission attracts the punishment in Section 3. A gang chart or earlier criminal case could not fill that legislative gap. The Court described the proceedings as legally unsustainable because criminal punishment requires a pre-existing, clearly defined offence. It expressly left the constitutional validity of the Act open and clarified that proceedings under other penal laws were unaffected.

## 2. Background
The Uttar Pradesh Gangsters and Anti-Social Activities (Prevention) Act, 1986 was enacted to deal with organised anti-social activity. Section 2(b) defines a “gang” through a group acting, singly or collectively, by violence, threat, intimidation, coercion or otherwise for listed anti-social objectives. Section 2(c) defines a “gangster” by reference to membership in, leadership of or assistance to such a gang. Section 3 then states the punishment for a gangster.

The appellants were prosecuted on the basis of gang charts referring to earlier cases. They argued that the statute did not specify a distinct prohibited act constituting an offence under the Act. The Supreme Court treated that objection as a question of legality, not merely evidence: a penal law must identify the conduct prohibited before it authorises punishment.

## 3. Timeline
- **1986**: Uttar Pradesh enacted the Gangsters and Anti-Social Activities (Prevention) Act.
- **2014–2018**: The underlying criminal cases and gang charts relied upon against the appellants arose during this period.
- **2024–2025**: The Allahabad High Court declined to interfere with the Gangsters Act proceedings challenged by the appellants.
- **20 August 2026**: The Supreme Court allowed the appeals and quashed the proceedings under Sections 2 and 3 of the Act.
- **After the judgment**: Independent proceedings for offences under other penal enactments remain governed by those enactments; the Court did not invalidate the 1986 Act as a whole.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Article 20(1)**: No person may be convicted except for violating a law in force at the time of the charged act, nor receive a greater penalty than the one then prescribed.
- **Principle of legality**: *Nullum crimen sine lege* means no crime without law; *nulla poena sine lege* means no punishment without law. The prohibited conduct and sanction must have a legislative basis.
- **Strict construction**: Penal statutes are construed strictly. Courts cannot cure the absence of an offence by implication, analogy or administrative practice.
- **Definition versus offence**: Defining “gang” and “gangster” identifies categories, but a criminal statute must also make specified conduct an offence. A status label is not by itself a complete actus reus.
- **Gang charts**: A gang chart may organise allegations or prior cases; it cannot substitute for the legislature’s duty to define criminal conduct.
- **Limited holding**: The Court quashed the proceedings before it. It did not rule on the Act’s overall constitutional validity and did not erase prosecutions under the IPC or other valid laws.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **Article 20 protections**: Article 20 contains protection against ex post facto criminal laws, double jeopardy and compelled self-incrimination.
- **Seventh Schedule**: “Public order” and “police” are State List subjects, while criminal law and criminal procedure are principally in the Concurrent List, subject to constitutional distribution rules.
- **Actus reus and mens rea**: Criminal responsibility ordinarily requires a prohibited act and the legally required mental element, though statutes may vary.
- **Rule of law**: Citizens must be able to know in advance what conduct attracts criminal liability; executive discretion cannot replace enacted law.
- **Judicial review**: Article 136 enables the Supreme Court, in its discretion, to grant special leave to appeal from judgments and orders of courts and tribunals.

## 7. CLAT Passage
A state statute defined a “gang” as a group pursuing listed anti-social objectives through violence, intimidation or related means. It defined a “gangster” by reference to membership in or assistance to such a group. A later section prescribed imprisonment for a gangster. Authorities initiated proceedings using gang charts that collected earlier criminal cases against the accused.

The Supreme Court held that criminal punishment cannot rest only on definitions of a group and a person. A penal statute must create an offence by identifying prohibited conduct. Neither a gang chart nor a prior accusation can supply a substantive offence that the legislature did not enact. This follows the principle of legality—there is no crime and no punishment without law—and the protection in Article 20(1). Penal statutes are interpreted strictly because liberty cannot depend on an offence invented by implication. The Court therefore quashed the proceedings under the state Act. It did not, however, decide the Act’s constitutional validity, and it allowed any independent proceedings under other penal statutes to continue according to law.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **Summary**: In 2026 INSC 894, the Supreme Court quashed UP Gangsters Act proceedings because definitions of “gang” and “gangster,” followed by punishment, did not amount to a legislatively defined offence.
- **Core rule**: Criminal liability requires prohibited conduct defined by law; executive documents cannot create crimes.
- **Constitution link**: Article 20(1) and the prohibition on ex post facto criminal liability.
- **Latin maxims**: *Nullum crimen sine lege* and *nulla poena sine lege*.
- **Statutory link**: Sections 2 and 3 of the Uttar Pradesh Gangsters and Anti-Social Activities (Prevention) Act, 1986.
- **Federal link**: Public order and police are State List subjects; criminal law and procedure are chiefly Concurrent List subjects.
- **Traps**: The Court did not hold that every gang-control law is unconstitutional.
- **Traps**: Quashing Gangsters Act proceedings did not automatically quash valid charges under other laws.
- **Mnemonic**: “Define the deed before punishing the person.”

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: Acquittal under every connected criminal law, invalidation of the entire Act, or a finding that states cannot legislate on public order.
- **Why they differ**: The judgment identified the lack of a substantive offence for the impugned Gangsters Act proceedings. It did not adjudicate all constitutional challenges, erase other penal charges, or deny state legislative competence over public order and police.
- **Memory clue**: **Definition ≠ offence**: naming a gang or gangster cannot replace a precisely enacted prohibited act.
