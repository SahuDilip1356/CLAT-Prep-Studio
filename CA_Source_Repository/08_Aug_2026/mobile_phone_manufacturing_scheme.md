# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 21 August 2026, the Union Government notified the **Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (MPMS)**, following Cabinet approval on 15 July 2026. The five-year scheme runs from FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31 with a total outlay of **₹62,500 crore**. It succeeds the Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Large Scale Electronics Manufacturing, whose scheduled period ended on 31 March 2026.

MPMS offers incentives linked to incremental eligible sales. The approved structure provides a base incentive ranging from **2.25% to 5%**, an additional incentive of up to **1.5%** for domestic sourcing of specified components, and an additional **3%** for eligible Indian-brand expenditure on design and research and development. The Government projects production of about **₹39 lakh crore** over five years and around **60,000 direct jobs**. These are policy targets, not guaranteed outcomes.

## 2. Background
India introduced the large-scale electronics PLI in 2020 to attract high-volume mobile-phone and component manufacturing. That programme rewarded incremental production and contributed to a shift from heavy import dependence toward assembly and exports from India. The next policy challenge is deeper value addition: more components, product design, intellectual property and supplier capabilities must be located domestically rather than limiting activity to final assembly.

MPMS retains output-linked incentives but adds distinct rewards for domestic sourcing and for design and R&D by Indian brands. It sits alongside, rather than replaces, other electronics policies such as the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme and the India Semiconductor Mission.

- **Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)**: Nodal ministry for implementation.

- **Domestic component suppliers**: Intended beneficiaries of the additional sourcing incentive.
- **Indian mobile-phone brands and design teams**: Eligible for a separate design-and-R&D incentive under the approved structure.
- **Parliament**: Authorises public expenditure through the annual financial process even when the policy is created by executive action.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- MPMS is an **executive industrial-policy scheme**, not a statute passed by Parliament. The Union's executive power under **Article 73** extends to matters on which Parliament may legislate.
- Public money must follow constitutional budget procedure. **Articles 112, 114 and 266** connect the annual financial statement, appropriation and withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund of India.
- Industrial development and inter-State trade engage Union legislative fields, while factories, labour compliance, land and local permissions may involve both Union and State legal frameworks.
- An output-linked incentive is conditional fiscal support. Notification does not create an unconditional right to payment; an applicant must satisfy eligibility, investment, sales and verification rules.
- Incentive design may also be assessed under the World Trade Organization's **Agreement on Subsidies and Countervailing Measures**. The existence of domestic-policy objectives does not by itself establish either WTO compliance or violation; the legal result depends on the precise measure and its operation.
- The scheme does not change customs duties, taxation or environmental permissions automatically. Those require their own legal instruments.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **PLI** means Production Linked Incentive: payment is tied to additional production or sales rather than simply reimbursing capital expenditure.
- **Value addition** is the increase in a product's value created by domestic inputs, processing, design and services.
- **Supply-chain resilience** means reducing vulnerability to disruption by diversifying suppliers, locations and critical inputs.
- MeitY is the nodal Union ministry for electronics and information-technology policy.
- **R&D** stands for research and development; product design and intellectual property can retain higher-value functions even when components cross borders.
- A fiscal year in India runs from **1 April to 31 March**.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme uses three incentive layers. A manufacturer may earn a base incentive on incremental eligible sales. Further support is available for sourcing specified components within India, and eligible Indian brands may receive an additional incentive for design and R&D expenditure. The structure reflects a policy shift from encouraging production volume alone toward deeper domestic value addition. Its five-year public outlay is ₹62,500 crore, while the Government's production and employment figures are forecasts. Because it is an executive scheme, firms must comply with the notified conditions; Cabinet approval does not guarantee payment to every producer. Parliament's financial authorisation remains necessary for expenditure from the Consolidated Fund.

## 11. Geo Card
- **Country**: India.

- **Strategic significance**: Mobile phones sit at the intersection of consumer electronics, semiconductors, telecommunications and global supply chains.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **MPMS vs PLI-LSEM**: MPMS is the successor scheme beginning FY 2026-27; PLI-LSEM was launched in 2020.
- **MPMS vs semiconductor incentives**: Phone manufacturing and semiconductor fabrication are related supply-chain policies but not the same scheme.
- **Outlay vs projected production**: ₹62,500 crore is the public outlay; ₹39 lakh crore is projected production.
- **Base vs add-on incentives**: Domestic sourcing and Indian-brand design/R&D are additional layers, not the base rate itself.
- **Notification vs automatic payment**: Firms must qualify and meet verified performance conditions.
