# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 3 August 2026, the Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee opened its fifth substantive session at United Nations Headquarters in New York. The session runs through 13 August. For the first time in this process, member states are expected to negotiate concrete draft language across three linked workstreams: a United Nations Framework Convention on International Tax Cooperation, an early protocol on taxing income from cross-border services, and an early protocol on preventing and resolving tax disputes. This is a negotiating milestone, not the adoption or entry into force of a treaty. The committee is working toward final texts for consideration by the UN General Assembly in 2027.

## 2. Background
International tax rules decide which country may tax income when a taxpayer, company, customer, asset, or service crosses borders. Traditional rules often give substantial weight to physical presence and the taxpayer's residence, while many developing and market countries seek greater rights to tax value connected with users, customers, services, and economic activity in their territories. The UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 79/235 on 24 December 2024, approving terms of reference and establishing an open-ended, member-state-led committee to negotiate a framework convention and two early protocols simultaneously. The process runs from 2025 to 2027. A framework convention would establish common objectives, principles, governance, and cooperation machinery; protocols can supply more detailed rules on particular subjects. The initiative exists alongside, and does not automatically replace, OECD-led tax work or the worldwide network of bilateral tax treaties.

## 3. Timeline
- **22 December 2023**: The UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 78/230, beginning the formal process for a UN framework convention on international tax cooperation.
- **24 December 2024**: General Assembly Resolution 79/235 adopted the terms of reference and established the intergovernmental negotiating committee.
- **3-6 February 2025**: The committee held its organizational session at UN Headquarters in New York.
- **4 August 2025**: The first substantive negotiating session began in New York.
- **13 February 2026**: The fourth substantive session concluded after work on the convention and the two early protocols.
- **3 August 2026**: The fifth session opened in New York, moving the process from concepts toward negotiation of concrete draft legal language.
- **13 August 2026**: Scheduled closing date of the fifth session.
- **2027**: The committee is due to submit final texts of the framework convention and two early protocols to the General Assembly for consideration.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **United Nations General Assembly**: Established the negotiating process through Resolution 79/235 and will consider the final texts.
- **Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee**: The open-ended, member-state-led body drafting the convention and two protocols.
- **UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs**: Its Financing for Sustainable Development Office serves as the substantive secretariat supporting the negotiations.
- **UN member states**: Negotiate the text and decide how taxing rights, cooperation, governance, and dispute mechanisms should be framed.
- **India and other developing economies**: Participate as states with a strong interest in the taxation of cross-border and digital economic activity in market jurisdictions.
- **OECD**: Runs a separate, established stream of international tax cooperation; the UN process must be distinguished from OECD/G20 initiatives.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
The negotiations concern the allocation of sovereign taxing powers among states. A convention could create a shared institutional framework, while protocols could contain more specific obligations. No draft now under negotiation is binding merely because it is discussed or approved by the committee; adoption, signature, consent to be bound, entry into force, and domestic implementation are legally distinct stages.

- **Article 265 of the Constitution**: No tax may be levied or collected in India except by authority of law; an international negotiation cannot itself impose a domestic tax.
- **Article 253 of the Constitution**: Parliament may make laws for implementing treaties, agreements, conventions, and decisions made at international conferences.
- **Article 51(c) of the Constitution**: The State should foster respect for international law and treaty obligations; it is a Directive Principle, not an independent charging provision for tax.
- **Union List, Entry 82**: Parliament has legislative competence over taxes on income other than agricultural income.
- **Section 90 of the Income-tax Act, 1961**: It enables the Central Government to enter into and notify agreements with foreign countries or specified territories on double-tax relief, avoidance, information exchange, and recovery of tax.
- **Fiscal sovereignty and coordination**: Source countries, residence countries, and market jurisdictions may assert competing claims over the same cross-border income, creating risks of both double taxation and non-taxation.
- **Dispute resolution and due process**: The second early protocol may shape how cross-border tax disputes are prevented and resolved, raising questions of transparency, consistency, taxpayer protection, and state consent.

## 6. Static GK Connection
The issue links the United Nations, treaty law, taxation concepts, and development finance.

- **Framework convention**: A treaty structure that sets broad principles, institutions, and procedures, often supplemented by more detailed protocols.
- **Protocol**: A related legal instrument that can add detailed rules or obligations to a wider treaty framework.
- **Source jurisdiction**: The country claiming taxing rights because income arises from activity, assets, customers, or payments connected with its territory.
- **Residence jurisdiction**: The country claiming taxing rights because the taxpayer is resident there.
- **Permanent establishment**: A treaty concept commonly used to decide when an enterprise has sufficient presence in another state for that state to tax specified business profits.
- **Double taxation**: The same income may be taxed by more than one jurisdiction; treaties can allocate taxing rights and provide relief.
- **Base erosion and profit shifting**: Arrangements that exploit gaps or mismatches in tax rules to shift profits away from the place of economic activity.
- **UN General Assembly**: The principal deliberative organ of the UN, composed of all 193 member states; its resolutions do not by themselves convert a draft convention into a binding treaty for every state.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The United Nations is negotiating a Framework Convention on International Tax Cooperation together with two early protocols. The fifth negotiating session, which opened in New York on 3 August 2026, is the first at which member states are expected to work across concrete draft legal language in all three workstreams. One protocol concerns income from cross-border services in an increasingly digitalized economy; the other concerns the prevention and resolution of tax disputes. The framework convention is intended to supply wider objectives, principles, governance, and cooperation machinery. The process responds partly to disagreements over which countries should tax profits when a business can earn revenue from customers in a jurisdiction without maintaining the traditional physical presence there. Residence countries, source countries, and market jurisdictions may claim different connections to the same income. Coordination can reduce both double taxation and non-taxation, but states disagree over the allocation of taxing rights. The committee is scheduled to complete final texts for General Assembly consideration in 2027. Those negotiations do not themselves impose a tax in India. Article 265 requires authority of law for any Indian tax, while Article 253 empowers Parliament to make laws implementing treaties and international decisions. A negotiated text, General Assembly consideration, state consent to be bound, entry into force, and domestic implementation must therefore be kept legally distinct.
