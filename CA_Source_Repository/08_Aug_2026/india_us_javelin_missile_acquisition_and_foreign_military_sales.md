# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 28 August 2026, the Indian Army signed a Letter of Offer and Acceptance (LOA) to acquire the Javelin anti-tank guided missile system through the United States Foreign Military Sales (FMS) process. The U.S. Mission in India described the LOA as a government-to-government agreement and said that it could lead to future industrial collaboration. The current official announcement did not disclose the final price, quantity or delivery schedule. A November 2025 U.S. congressional notification had described a *possible* package of 100 Javelin rounds, one fly-to-buy missile, 25 command launch units and support at a maximum estimated value of US$45.7 million. That earlier ceiling is useful background but is not proof of the final 2026 contract terms. Co-production also remains a possibility for future discussion, not a term established by the public announcement.

## 2. Background
The Javelin is a man-portable, medium-range, fire-and-forget anti-tank guided missile produced by a joint venture of RTX and Lockheed Martin. Its imaging-infrared seeker locks on before launch, allowing the operator to relocate after firing. It can use a top-attack profile against armoured vehicles and a direct-attack mode against other targets. India and the United States had discussed acquisition and possible production arrangements for the system for many years, while India also pursued Israeli Spike missiles and the indigenous DRDO Man-Portable Anti-Tank Guided Missile. The 2026 LOA converts the U.S. offer into an accepted FMS case; it does not mean that delivery, induction or Indian manufacture has already occurred.

Under FMS, the foreign partner purchases from the U.S. Government rather than contracting directly with the manufacturer. The partner first sends a Letter of Request. After U.S. policy, security and—where applicable—Congressional review, the U.S. offers an LOA specifying the proposed articles and services. Acceptance creates the government-to-government agreement, after which procurement, payment, delivery, training and end-use monitoring are implemented.

## 3. Timeline
- **2009**: Indian soldiers used Javelin during Exercise Yudh Abhyas; acquisition discussions followed.
- **2016**: The United States recognised India as a Major Defense Partner.
- **February 2025**: India and the United States publicly signalled plans to pursue Javelin procurement and co-production discussions.
- **19 November 2025**: The U.S. Defense Security Cooperation Agency notified Congress of a possible Javelin sale to India, with a maximum estimated value of US$45.7 million.
- **October 2025**: India and the United States signed a ten-year framework for their major defence partnership.
- **28 August 2026**: The Indian Army signed the Javelin LOA through the FMS route; the U.S. Mission announced the milestone.
- **After acceptance**: Case implementation, contracting, financial milestones, logistics and delivery remain separate stages.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
The acquisition operates through executive action, legislative finance control and the exporting State's arms-transfer law.

- **Articles 53 and 73 of the Constitution of India**: Union executive power is exercised in the President's name and extends to matters on which Parliament may legislate, including defence.
- **Article 246 read with Union List Entry 1**: Defence of India and the armed forces fall within Parliament's exclusive legislative field.
- **Articles 112-114**: Defence expenditure remains subject to the annual financial statement, parliamentary grants and appropriation from the Consolidated Fund of India.
- **Article 149**: The Comptroller and Auditor General's duties support audit accountability for public expenditure.
- **Arms Export Control Act, United States**: Authorises and regulates FMS; DSCA identifies the LOA as the legal instrument for the government-to-government transfer.
- **Congressional notification**: A proposed maximum quantity and value notified before a sale is not the same thing as the final accepted quantity and price.
- **End-use and retransfer controls**: FMS articles remain subject to agreed use, security and transfer conditions.
- **Co-production**: A diplomatic statement that a purchase “opens the door” to co-production does not itself create a manufacturing licence, transfer technology or place production in India.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **FMS versus Direct Commercial Sales**: FMS is government-to-government; a direct commercial sale is negotiated with a company but requires relevant export authorisation.
- **LOA**: Letter of Offer and Acceptance—the government-to-government agreement used in an FMS case.
- **LOR**: Letter of Request—the foreign partner's request that begins the formal FMS process.
- **DSCA**: A U.S. Department of Defense agency that administers security-cooperation programmes.
- **Javelin**: A fire-and-forget anti-tank guided missile with reusable command launch equipment and disposable missile rounds.
- **Top attack**: A flight profile intended to strike the relatively less protected upper surface of an armoured vehicle.
- **Major Defense Partner**: A U.S. designation granted to India in 2016; it is not a mutual-defence treaty.
- **Induction**: Entry into operational service; signing an LOA precedes delivery and induction.

## 7. CLAT Passage
An official announcement that a country has “signed a deal” can conceal several legally distinct stages. In the United States Foreign Military Sales process, the purchasing country first submits a request. The United States then conducts policy, security and, where required, Congressional review. A public congressional notification normally states the highest estimated quantity and value that may be offered; it is not a final invoice. A Letter of Offer and Acceptance sets out the government-to-government offer, and acceptance creates the FMS agreement. Implementation, payment, contracting, delivery, training and end-use monitoring follow. India’s 28 August 2026 acceptance of a Javelin LOA therefore marked a completed procurement milestone, but it did not establish that the missiles had been delivered or inducted. Nor did it make future co-production automatic. The 2025 U.S. notification had referred to a possible package of 100 rounds, one fly-to-buy missile, 25 launch units and related support at an estimated ceiling of US$45.7 million. Because the 2026 public release did not disclose the accepted case's price or quantity, the ceiling should not be presented as a confirmed final amount. In India, executive authorities may conduct defence procurement, while Parliament retains constitutional control over grants and appropriation. The arrangement illustrates how executive diplomacy, public finance and exporting-State controls can operate together without turning every announced possibility into a present legal commitment.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: DSCA notification, LOA, delivery, induction and co-production.
- **Why they differ**: Notification permits review of a proposed ceiling; LOA acceptance forms the FMS case; delivery transfers equipment; induction places it in service; co-production requires a separate industrial arrangement.
- **Frequently confused with**: Javelin and Excalibur.
- **Why they differ**: Javelin is an anti-tank guided missile; Excalibur is a precision-guided artillery projectile covered by a separate proposed sale.
- **Memory clue**: “Ceiling is not cost; signature is not delivery.”
