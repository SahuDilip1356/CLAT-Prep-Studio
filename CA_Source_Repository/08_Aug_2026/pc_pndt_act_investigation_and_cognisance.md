# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 20 August 2026, the Supreme Court delivered its judgment in **State of Uttar Pradesh v. Brij Pal Singh**, 2026 INSC 901. It held that police cannot register an FIR and independently investigate offences created only by the Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (Prohibition of Sex Selection) Act, 1994 (PC-PNDT Act).

The Act assigns investigation to the statutory **Appropriate Authority**, and Section 28 permits a court to take cognisance only on a complaint by the Appropriate Authority, an authorised officer or an eligible person or organisation after statutory notice. A police chargesheet cannot become the foundation for cognisance of a PC-PNDT-only offence. Police may nevertheless investigate independent IPC or BNS offences and may provide supplementary operational assistance under the Appropriate Authority’s supervision.

## 2. Background
Parliament first enacted the Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (Regulation and Prevention of Misuse) Act in 1994 to regulate diagnostic techniques and prevent their misuse for sex determination leading to female foeticide. The 2003 amendments expanded the law to prohibit sex selection before as well as after conception and renamed it the PC-PNDT Act.

The Act creates a specialised enforcement design. Appropriate Authorities are appointed under Section 17. Section 17(4) empowers them to investigate complaints, search and seize records and take legal action. Rule 18A requires states and Union Territories to ensure effective implementation and directs that police involvement be avoided as far as possible except for offences under other laws. Section 28 restricts the manner in which a court may take cognisance.

## 3. Timeline
- **1994**: Parliament enacted the Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques Act.
- **1 January 1996**: The central enactment came into force.
- **2002–2003**: Parliament amended the law to cover pre-conception sex selection; the amended title became the PC-PNDT Act.
- **2014**: Rule 18A was inserted to standardise monitoring and enforcement, including the instruction to avoid police involvement as far as possible for PC-PNDT enforcement.
- **2025**: The Supreme Court granted leave in the appeal arising from prosecution in Uttar Pradesh.
- **20 August 2026**: In 2026 INSC 901, the Supreme Court clarified the exclusive statutory route for investigating PC-PNDT offences and taking cognisance.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Supreme Court of India**: Interpreted the enforcement and cognisance provisions of the central Act.
- **Central and State Appropriate Authorities**: Statutory bodies appointed under Section 17 to supervise implementation, investigate complaints and initiate prosecution.
- **Advisory Committees**: Assist Appropriate Authorities under the Act; they do not replace the Authority’s statutory functions.
- **Police**: May investigate separate IPC/BNS offences and assist with protection, tracing, preservation or forensic tasks, but cannot displace the Appropriate Authority for PC-PNDT-only offences.
- **Magistrates**: Must respect the statutory bar on cognisance in Section 28.
- **Genetic counselling centres, laboratories and clinics**: Regulated establishments required to follow registration and record-keeping duties.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Special law controls its procedure**: When Parliament assigns investigation and prosecution to a designated authority, the general police process cannot override that special design.
- **Section 17(4)**: The Appropriate Authority investigates complaints, takes immediate action, searches and seizes records and pursues legal proceedings.
- **Rule 18A(3)(iv)**: Police involvement is to be avoided as far as possible for implementing the Act; police retain their ordinary role for independent offences under other laws.
- **Section 28 cognisance bar**: A court may take cognisance only on the specified statutory complaints. A police report is not one of them.
- **Two procedural tracks**: The same facts may disclose both PC-PNDT offences and separate IPC/BNS offences. The Appropriate Authority handles the former; police may investigate the latter.
- **Supplementary assistance**: Police can support the Appropriate Authority with security, tracing persons, digital or forensic help and preservation of evidence, without assuming statutory control.
- **Constitutional context**: The Act advances equality and dignity by combating sex selection and the systemic devaluation of girls, while criminal procedure remains governed by enacted safeguards.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **Full form**: PC-PNDT means Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (Prohibition of Sex Selection).
- **Purpose**: Regulation of prenatal diagnostic techniques and prohibition of their misuse for sex selection or disclosure of fetal sex.
- **Article 14**: Equality before law and equal protection of laws.
- **Article 15(1)**: Prohibits State discrimination on specified grounds including sex; Article 15(3) permits special provisions for women and children.
- **Article 21**: Protects life and personal liberty, including dignity under constitutional jurisprudence.
- **Cognisance**: The judicial act of taking notice of an alleged offence for proceeding according to law; it is distinct from investigation.
- **Special versus general law**: A specific statutory procedure ordinarily prevails over a general procedure for the field it expressly occupies.

## 7. CLAT Passage
A central social-welfare statute prohibits sex selection and regulates prenatal diagnostic facilities. It appoints an Appropriate Authority to investigate complaints, search and seize records and initiate prosecution. Another provision says that a court may take cognisance only on a complaint by that Authority, an authorised officer, or an eligible person or organisation after giving statutory notice. Rules framed under the Act say that police involvement should be avoided as far as possible, except for offences under other laws.

The Supreme Court held that police cannot register an FIR and independently investigate an offence created only by this special Act. Nor may a magistrate take cognisance of such an offence merely on a police chargesheet. If the same episode also discloses an independent offence under the general penal code, police may investigate that offence. They may also assist the Appropriate Authority with security, tracing, digital or forensic support and evidence preservation, but that assistance remains supplemental. The ruling therefore separates statutory control from practical cooperation rather than excluding police from every related task.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **Summary**: In 2026 INSC 901, the Supreme Court held that the Appropriate Authority—not police—investigates PC-PNDT-only offences, and Section 28 bars cognisance on a police chargesheet.
- **Core provisions**: Section 17(4), Rule 18A(3)(iv) and Section 28.
- **Two tracks**: Appropriate Authority for PC-PNDT offences; police for independent IPC/BNS offences.
- **Assistance rule**: Police may provide operational support but cannot take over the special statutory investigation.
- **Purpose**: Prevent sex selection and regulate diagnostic techniques and facilities.
- **Constitution links**: Articles 14, 15 and 21.
- **Traps**: The judgment did not legalise sex determination or weaken registration and record duties.
- **Traps**: It did not prohibit police from investigating every offence arising from the same incident.
- **Mnemonic**: “17 investigates; 28 opens the court gate.”

## 11. Geo Card
- **Location**: The appeal arose from Uttar Pradesh, but the PC-PNDT Act is a central law applicable across India.
- **Capital**: New Delhi is the seat of the Supreme Court and the Union government that enacted the national framework.
- **Strategic significance**: Enforcement depends on state and Union Territory Appropriate Authorities, making administrative capacity and accurate clinic records important nationwide.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: A total prohibition on police assistance, abolition of all FIRs connected with clinic activity, or an exemption from the Act’s substantive duties.
- **Why they differ**: The judgment allocates statutory roles. It preserves police investigation of independent general-law offences and permits supplemental assistance, while requiring PC-PNDT prosecution to follow the Act’s special complaint and cognisance route.
- **Memory clue**: **Authority leads; police support; Section 28 controls cognisance.**
