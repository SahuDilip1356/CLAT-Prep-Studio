# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 31 August 2026, Bihar and Jharkhand signed a memorandum of understanding in New Delhi to settle their long-running dispute over the share of Sone River water inherited from undivided Bihar. The agreement was signed in the presence of Union Home Minister Amit Shah, Bihar Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary, Jharkhand Chief Minister Hemant Soren and Union Jal Shakti Minister C. R. Patil. The Union government described it as ending a 25-year dispute and enabling irrigation, drinking-water and regional development benefits.

Independent reports state that undivided Bihar's 7.75 million acre-feet allocation under the 1973 Bansagar Agreement is now divided as 5.75 MAF for Bihar and 2.00 MAF for Jharkhand. The official press release confirms the settlement and signing but does not reproduce the full MoU text or those detailed allocation clauses; the figures should therefore be attributed to the corroborating reports unless the MoU is later published.

## 2. Background
The Sone is a major right-bank tributary of the Ganga. The 1973 Bansagar Agreement allocated Sone basin water among Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and undivided Bihar. When Jharkhand was created from Bihar on 15 November 2000, the two successor States disputed how undivided Bihar's share should be divided.

The dispute affected planning for the Indrapuri reservoir project in Bihar, earlier referred to as the Kadwan reservoir project. A 2025 Rajya Sabha answer recorded that the Central Water Commission returned the project's preliminary report in March 2022 because the successor-State allocation and no-objection issues remained unresolved. The matter was also considered through the Eastern Zonal Council process before the 2026 political settlement.

## 3. Timeline
- **16 September 1973**: Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and undivided Bihar concluded the Bansagar Agreement for sharing Sone waters.
- **15 November 2000**: Jharkhand was created under the Bihar Reorganisation Act, 2000, creating the need to divide undivided Bihar's water share and project interests.
- **December 2018**: Bihar submitted a preliminary project report for the Indrapuri reservoir project to the Central Water Commission.
- **March 2022**: The Central Water Commission returned the report pending resolution of the allocation and co-basin State no-objection issues.
- **10 July 2024**: The dispute was discussed at the Standing Committee of the Eastern Zonal Council.
- **31 August 2026**: Bihar and Jharkhand signed the MoU in New Delhi; the Union government announced that the 25-year dispute had been settled.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Government of Bihar**: Successor State seeking certainty for irrigation and the proposed Indrapuri reservoir; represented at signing by Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary.
- **Government of Jharkhand**: Successor State receiving a defined share; represented by Chief Minister Hemant Soren.
- **Union Ministry of Jal Shakti and Central Water Commission**: Provide technical scrutiny and Union-level coordination for water-resource projects.
- **Union Ministry of Home Affairs**: Supports inter-State coordination and administers zonal council mechanisms; Amit Shah witnessed the signing.
- **Eastern Zonal Council**: A coordinating forum under the States Reorganisation Act, 1956, covering Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha and West Bengal.
- **Bansagar Control Board**: Coordinates implementation of the 1973 agreement and the Bansagar multipurpose project framework.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Article 262**: Parliament may provide for adjudication of disputes over an inter-State river or river valley and may exclude court jurisdiction in such disputes.
- **Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956**: Provides the statutory tribunal route when negotiation fails and the statutory conditions are met. This MoU is a negotiated settlement, not a tribunal award.
- **Bihar Reorganisation Act, 2000**: Sections 78–82 address rights, liabilities and arrangements concerning water-resource projects after reorganisation, including Union directions and possible management structures.
- **Entry 17, State List**: Water, irrigation and canals are ordinarily State subjects, subject to Entry 56 of the Union List.
- **Entry 56, Union List**: Parliament may regulate and develop inter-State rivers and river valleys to the extent declared expedient in the public interest.
- **Cooperative federalism**: The settlement illustrates executive negotiation among States with Union facilitation. It does not amend Article 262 or replace the statutory tribunal system for other disputes.
- **Agreement versus adjudication**: A consensual MoU draws authority from agreement and applicable statutes; it is legally distinct from a judicial judgment or an inter-State river tribunal award.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- **Sone River**: Rises near the Amarkantak plateau and flows north-east through central and eastern India before joining the Ganga near Patna.
- **River classification**: The Sone is a right-bank tributary of the Ganga.
- **Bansagar Dam**: Built on the Sone in Madhya Pradesh and named after the Sanskrit scholar Bana Bhatt.
- **Successor States**: Jharkhand became India's 28th State on 15 November 2000.
- **Zonal Councils**: Statutory bodies established under the States Reorganisation Act, 1956; the Union Home Minister is their common Chairperson.
- **MAF**: One million acre-feet is a volume measure equal to about 1.233 billion cubic metres.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The Constitution places water primarily in the State List, but subjects that competence to Parliament's power over inter-State rivers under Union List Entry 56. Article 262 permits Parliament to establish a special adjudicatory framework for inter-State river disputes. Parliament exercised that power through the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956. Yet not every river disagreement must end in a tribunal: States may negotiate, and the Union may facilitate a settlement through administrative and intergovernmental institutions.

That route produced the Bihar–Jharkhand Sone River MoU of 31 August 2026. The dispute arose because the 1973 Bansagar Agreement allocated water to undivided Bihar, but did not itself settle the division between Bihar and Jharkhand after the latter State was created in 2000. The unresolved allocation also delayed technical processing of Bihar's proposed Indrapuri reservoir. The new MoU records an agreed division, reported as 5.75 MAF for Bihar and 2.00 MAF for Jharkhand, and is expected to unlock irrigation and drinking-water planning. It is an example of cooperative federalism: political agreement resolves a successor-State problem within the larger constitutional and statutory framework. It is not a tribunal award and does not remove Article 262 remedies for future disputes.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Sone/Son spelling**: Both forms refer to the same river; official documents often use “Sone”.
- **MoU vs tribunal award**: The settlement was negotiated, not adjudicated under the 1956 Act.
- **Official confirmation vs detailed allocation**: The PIB confirms settlement; the 5.75/2.00 MAF figures come from corroborating independent reports because the full MoU was not publicly reproduced in the sources reviewed.
- **Article 262 vs Entry 56**: Article 262 concerns dispute adjudication; Entry 56 concerns Union regulation and development of inter-State rivers after a parliamentary declaration.
- **Eastern Zonal Council vs Inter-State Council**: They are different institutions under different legal bases.
