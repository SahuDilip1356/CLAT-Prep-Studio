# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 21 August 2026, a three-judge Bench of the Supreme Court in **Bhumika Trust v. Union of India, 2026 INSC 904** modified the entry conditions for recruitment as Civil Judge (Junior Division). The majority, speaking through Chief Justice Surya Kant with Justice Augustine George Masih, reduced the minimum pre-recruitment practice requirement from three years to one year and created a transition regime. Justice K. Vinod Chandran dissented.

For recruitment processes notified between 20 May 2025 and 31 March 2027, eligible law graduates may apply without producing a practice certificate. A selected candidate is to be treated as a trainee judicial officer and must complete one year at the State Judicial Academy followed by a structured one-year clerkship: six months with the District/Higher Judiciary and six months with a sitting High Court judge. For recruitment notifications issued on or after 1 April 2027, a candidate must first complete one year of actual practice before undergoing the same training and clerkship. The Court directed State Governments and High Courts to amend their rules within three months and said the scheme may be reviewed after five years.

## 2. Background
The dispute arose in the continuing proceedings concerning conditions of service in the subordinate judiciary. In **All India Judges' Association v. Union of India (1993)**, the Supreme Court accepted the recommendation that a person should ordinarily have three years' practice before entering judicial service. In 2002, after the Shetty Commission's recommendations, the Court removed that requirement so that fresh law graduates could compete. On 20 May 2025, the Court restored a three-year practice requirement prospectively, prompting challenges and requests for modification.

The 2026 majority treated courtroom exposure as valuable but found that an inflexible three-year bar could exclude capable graduates and create transition problems. Its alternative combines shorter pre-entry practice with longer institutional training. The dissent would not have substituted the recently restored norm so quickly.

## 3. Timeline
- **1993**: The Supreme Court in the first All India Judges' Association decision supports three years' practice for entry-level judicial appointments.
- **2002**: The Court removes the practice requirement following the Shetty Commission process, allowing fresh law graduates to compete.
- **20 May 2025**: The Supreme Court prospectively restores a three-year minimum practice condition.
- **20 May 2025-31 March 2027**: The 2026 judgment treats this as the transition window; applicants need no practice certificate but selected candidates must complete the two-stage training structure.
- **21 August 2026**: Bhumika Trust v. Union of India reduces the future pre-entry requirement to one year by a 2:1 majority.
- **From 1 April 2027**: New recruitment notifications must require one year of actual practice, followed by one year at a Judicial Academy and one year of structured clerkship.
- **By 21 November 2026**: State Governments and High Courts are expected to align their recruitment rules within the Court's three-month period.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Article 234** governs appointments of persons other than district judges to a State's judicial service. The Governor makes appointments according to rules after consultation with the State Public Service Commission and the High Court.
- **Article 235** vests control over district and subordinate courts in the High Court. This explains why training, evaluation and service rules cannot be designed by the executive alone.
- **Articles 233-237** form the constitutional chapter on subordinate courts. Article 233's seven-year advocacy qualification concerns a person appointed as a district judge from the Bar; it is not the qualification for Civil Judge (Junior Division) recruitment under Article 234.
- **Article 141** makes the law declared by the Supreme Court binding on all courts. The directions also operate within the Court's continuing institutional supervision of judicial-service reform.
- The ruling illustrates a judicial-administration choice between **prior professional exposure** and **structured post-selection training**. It does not abolish practical preparation: it redistributes it across one year of practice, one academy year and one clerkship year for future entrants.
- Judicial independence is part of the Constitution's basic structure, while recruitment to the subordinate judiciary is a shared constitutional process involving Governors, Public Service Commissions and High Courts.

## 6. Static GK Connection
- The subordinate judiciary is dealt with in **Part VI, Chapter VI of the Constitution (Articles 233-237)**.
- The **First National Judicial Pay Commission**, commonly called the **Shetty Commission**, was constituted in 1996 under Justice K. Jagannatha Shetty.
- A Civil Judge (Junior Division), often also exercising Judicial Magistrate First Class powers under State arrangements, is an entry-level judicial officer; a district judge is a higher constitutional category under Article 233.
- State Judicial Academies train judicial officers; the National Judicial Academy is located in **Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh**.
- A majority judgment is binding; a dissent records a contrary legal view but does not control the result.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The Supreme Court modified the entry route to the civil judiciary after balancing two competing concerns. Courtroom practice can expose a future judge to procedure, professional ethics and litigants' realities. Yet a long compulsory waiting period may deter strong graduates and does not by itself guarantee useful experience. The majority therefore adopted a phased model. During the transition period ending on 31 March 2027, law graduates may compete without a practice certificate, but successful candidates must undergo a year at a State Judicial Academy and a year of structured clerkship. For notifications issued from 1 April 2027, candidates must additionally have one year of actual practice before selection. The scheme preserves the constitutional role of High Courts in controlling the subordinate judiciary and requires States and High Courts to amend their rules. The dissent questioned changing a practice requirement that the Court had restored only recently.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **30-second summary**: A 2:1 Supreme Court majority reduced the pre-entry Civil Judge practice requirement from three years to one. Transition applicants through 31 March 2027 need no certificate; future recruitment from 1 April 2027 requires one actual practice year. Both routes lead to one academy year and one structured clerkship year.
- **Case**: Bhumika Trust v. Union of India, 2026 INSC 904, decided 21 August 2026.
- **Constitution map**: Article 234 = appointments below district judge; Article 235 = High Court control; Article 233 = district judges.
- **Institution map**: Governor + State PSC + High Court frame and implement recruitment; State Judicial Academy trains selectees.
- **Exam trap**: Do not say the judgment removed practical experience. It shortened pre-entry practice and expanded structured post-selection experience.
- **Date trap**: 31 March 2027 closes the transition; 1 April 2027 begins the one-year actual-practice condition for new notifications.
- **Mnemonic**: **1-1-1 after 2027** — one practice year, one academy year, one clerkship year.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Article 233 vs Article 234**: Article 233 covers district judges; Article 234 covers other State judicial-service appointments.
- **Pre-entry practice vs post-selection training**: One is professional practice before application; the others occur after selection.
- **Transition vs permanent rule**: Deemed eligibility applies only to the specified transition cohort, not to notifications from 1 April 2027.
- **Dissent vs holding**: Justice Chandran's dissent is important reasoning but the two-judge majority controls the result.
- **National vs State Judicial Academy**: The directions specify State Judicial Academy training; the National Judicial Academy at Bhopal serves broader judicial education functions.
