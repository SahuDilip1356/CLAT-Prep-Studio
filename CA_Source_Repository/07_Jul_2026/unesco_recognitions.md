# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 25 July 2026, the World Heritage Committee inscribed the Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath in Uttar Pradesh on the UNESCO World Heritage List at its 48th session in Busan, Republic of Korea. UNESCO and India’s Ministry of Culture both identify it as India’s 45th World Heritage property. The serial property has two component parts: Chaukhandi Stupa and the Archaeological Remains of Sarnath, which include the Dhamekh Stupa, Dharmarajika Stupa, Mulagandhakuti Vihara, and Ashokan Pillar remains.

## 2. Background
Sarnath, near Varanasi, is associated with the Buddha’s first sermon after enlightenment and the beginning of the Buddhist sangha. The site developed through royal, monastic, and lay patronage and contains remains spanning roughly the fifth–fourth centuries BCE to the twelfth century CE. It entered India’s World Heritage Tentative List in 1998, and India submitted the nomination for the 2025–26 cycle. Under the 1972 World Heritage Convention, the Committee inscribes properties that possess Outstanding Universal Value and satisfy authenticity, integrity, protection, and management requirements.

## 3. Timeline
- **3 July 1998**: Sarnath entered India’s Tentative List for possible World Heritage nomination.
- **January 2025**: India submitted the nomination for the 2025–26 cycle.
- **24–29 September 2025**: An ICOMOS technical evaluation mission examined the nominated property.
- **25 July 2026**: The 48th World Heritage Committee session inscribed Sarnath, making it India’s 45th listed property.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **World Heritage Committee**: The intergovernmental body that decides inscriptions and monitors implementation of the Convention.
- **UNESCO World Heritage Centre**: Serves as the Convention’s secretariat and coordinates nominations and monitoring.
- **Archaeological Survey of India**: India’s nodal agency for World Heritage matters and the principal conservation authority at Sarnath.
- **ICOMOS**: The advisory body that evaluates cultural-property nominations for the Committee.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
World Heritage inscription is not a transfer of sovereignty to UNESCO. The property remains under Indian jurisdiction, while India accepts international conservation and reporting responsibilities under the Convention.

- **World Heritage Convention, 1972**: creates the List and obliges States Parties to identify, protect, conserve, present, and transmit listed heritage to future generations.
- **Article 49 of the Constitution of India**: directs the State to protect monuments, places, and objects of national importance from spoliation, disfigurement, destruction, removal, disposal, or export.
- **Article 51A(f) of the Constitution of India**: makes preservation of the rich heritage of India’s composite culture a fundamental duty of citizens.
- **Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958**: supplies the central statutory framework for protection of monuments and archaeological sites declared nationally important.

## 6. Static GK Connection
Sarnath links Buddhist history, Mauryan symbolism, India’s national emblem, and the machinery of UNESCO heritage recognition.

- **First sermon**: Sarnath is traditionally associated with Dharmachakra Pravartana, the “Turning of the Wheel of Dharma.”
- **Buddhist sangha**: the first sermon to the initial disciples is associated with the formation of the monastic community.
- **Lion Capital of Ashoka**: excavated at Sarnath and adopted as India’s State Emblem; the wheel from it appears on the national flag.
- **Tentative List**: inclusion is a prerequisite before a State Party may nominate a property to the World Heritage List.
- **Three property types**: the Convention recognises cultural, natural, and mixed properties.

## 7. CLAT Passage
At its 48th session in Busan, the World Heritage Committee inscribed the Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath as India’s 45th World Heritage property. The serial nomination contains Chaukhandi Stupa and the Archaeological Remains of Sarnath. UNESCO describes Sarnath as the place where the Buddha met his first disciples and delivered his first sermon after enlightenment. India placed the site on its Tentative List in 1998 and later submitted a formal nomination, which underwent evaluation including an ICOMOS mission. Inscription is based on Outstanding Universal Value and does not make UNESCO the owner of the property. India remains responsible for conservation and management under domestic law and its obligations as a party to the 1972 Convention. In India, Article 49 directs the State to protect monuments of national importance, while Article 51A(f) asks citizens to preserve the country’s composite cultural heritage.
