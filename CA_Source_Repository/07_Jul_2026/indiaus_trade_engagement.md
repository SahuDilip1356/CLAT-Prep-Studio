# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 25 July 2026, India’s Ministry of Commerce and Industry responded to final action by the United States Trade Representative under section 301 of the US Trade Act of 1974. The United States placed India in the lower 10% additional-duty tier after initially proposing 12.5%. The Indian statement estimated that exemptions leave about 45% of India’s exports to the United States outside the additional duty, while the remaining 55% attract the new 10% levy. India said negotiations on the bilateral trade agreement and sector-specific questions, including textiles, would continue.

## 2. Background
Section 301 authorises the USTR to investigate and respond to foreign acts, policies, or practices considered unreasonable, discriminatory, or burdensome to US commerce. In March 2026, USTR began investigations covering 60 economies over the absence or enforcement of prohibitions on imports made with forced labour. The final action followed consultations, public comments, and hearings. India’s placement in the 10% tier reflects USTR’s categories for economies that have a forced-labour import prohibition, have committed to one through a reciprocal trade agreement, or operate a partial regime that prevents some forced-labour goods. This tariff is distinct from ordinary most-favoured-nation customs duties and from sectoral section 232 measures.

## 3. Timeline
- **2 June 2026**: USTR proposed a 12.5% additional duty for India while seeking comments on responsive action in the forced-labour investigations.
- **7–9 July 2026**: USTR held public hearings on the proposed action after receiving written submissions.
- **23 July 2026**: USTR announced final Section 301 action covering 60 economies and placed India in the 10% tier.
- **25 July 2026**: India’s Commerce Ministry stated that roughly 45% of exports were exempt, 55% would face the additional levy, and bilateral trade negotiations would continue.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Office of the United States Trade Representative**: Conducted the Section 301 investigations and imposed the final responsive duties.
- **India’s Ministry of Commerce and Industry**: Represented India during consultations and issued the official Indian assessment on 25 July.
- **Section 301 Committee**: Received comments, conducted hearings, and advised USTR on the action.
- **World Trade Organization**: Supplies the wider rules-based trade framework, including the most-favoured-nation principle, though the challenged measure is US domestic trade action.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
The issue is principally one of international economic regulation and US statutory trade power rather than a change in Indian constitutional law. It is nevertheless useful for distinguishing domestic tariff authority from treaty obligations and diplomatic negotiation.

- **Section 301, US Trade Act of 1974**: authorises investigation of foreign practices considered unjustifiable, unreasonable, or discriminatory and permits responsive trade action.
- **Section 232 measures**: are national-security-based sectoral duties; Indian steel, aluminium, and auto parts already covered by them are excluded from the additional Section 301 duty.
- **Most-favoured-nation duty**: is the ordinary tariff treatment applied under the importing state’s schedule; Reuters reported that the new levy applies in addition to standard US MFN tariffs.
- **Article 253 of the Constitution of India**: gives Parliament power to legislate for implementing treaties and international agreements, while treaty negotiation remains an executive function.

## 6. Static GK Connection
The candidate should connect the tariff news to the institutions and vocabulary of world trade rather than treating every customs measure as the same kind of duty.

- **USTR**: is an executive office of the United States responsible for developing and coordinating US international trade policy.
- **Ad valorem tariff**: is calculated as a percentage of the customs value of the imported product.
- **MFN principle**: generally requires a WTO member to extend like tariff treatment to like products of other members, subject to recognised exceptions.
- **Forced labour**: is also addressed by International Labour Organization conventions, linking labour rights with supply-chain and trade policy.
- **Product exemptions**: mean the headline tariff rate does not automatically apply to every export line from the affected economy.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The United States Trade Representative completed investigations into 60 economies concerning prohibitions on imports made with forced labour. It imposed an additional 10% duty on India, lower than the 12.5% initially proposed. USTR explained that the 10% tier covers economies that prohibit forced-labour imports, commit to such a prohibition through a reciprocal trade agreement, or operate a partial regime preventing certain forced-labour goods. India’s Commerce Ministry stated that generic pharmaceuticals, smartphones, and specified products that currently attract no additional duty remain outside the new levy. Products already subject to section 232 measures, including steel, aluminium, and auto parts, are also excluded. India estimated that these exemptions leave 45% of its exports outside the additional duty and expose 55% to it. The new Section 301 duty operates separately from ordinary most-favoured-nation tariffs. India said it would continue talks on a bilateral trade agreement and unresolved sector-specific matters such as textiles.

### Questions
1. **Which conclusion is best supported by the passage?**:
   - (A) Every Indian export to the United States now attracts a single 10% tariff.
   - (B) The new duty is product-sensitive, and some exports remain outside it because of exemptions or other tariff regimes.
   - (C) Section 232 duties were abolished when the Section 301 action began.
   - (D) India ended bilateral trade negotiations after the final measure.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: The passage expressly identifies exempt product groups and products already covered by section 232, so the 10% duty does not apply uniformly to every export.
2. **Why was India placed in the 10% tier rather than the initially proposed 12.5% tier, according to the stated framework?**:
   - (A) India withdrew from the WTO.
   - (B) India met one of USTR’s lower-tier conditions concerning forced-labour import restrictions or commitments.
   - (C) India agreed to treat section 232 duties as MFN tariffs.
   - (D) India prohibited all textile exports to the United States.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: USTR’s lower tier covers economies with a prohibition, a reciprocal-agreement commitment, or a partial regime preventing certain forced-labour goods.

## 10. One-Pager Revision
- **Summary**: USTR placed India in a lower 10% Section 301 forced-labour tariff tier; India says exemptions shield about 45% of exports and bilateral trade talks will continue.
- **Traps**: Do not say the 10% levy covers every Indian export; exemptions and section 232 exclusions matter.
- **Traps**: Do not confuse Section 301 trade retaliation, section 232 national-security duties, and ordinary MFN customs tariffs.

• Scope estimate: 45% exempt and 55% covered, according to India’s Commerce Ministry.
• USTR investigation: 60 economies and forced-labour import prohibitions.
• Excluded examples: generic pharmaceuticals, smartphones, and products already under section 232.
• Continuing track: India–US bilateral trade agreement negotiations, including textiles.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: US section 232 sectoral duties and standard WTO/MFN tariff rates.
- **Why they differ**: Section 301 responds to specified foreign practices, section 232 addresses national-security imports, and MFN rates are ordinary scheduled customs duties.
- **Memory clue**: “301 is practice; 232 is security; MFN is schedule.”
