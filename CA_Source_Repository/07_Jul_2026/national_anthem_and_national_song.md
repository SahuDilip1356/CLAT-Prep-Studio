# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
The Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026 was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 24 July 2026. The introduced text would replace section 3 of the 1971 Act so that intentionally preventing the singing of either the Indian National Anthem or the National Song, or causing disturbance to an assembly engaged in such singing, is punishable with imprisonment up to three years, a fine, or both. The existing section expressly protects the National Anthem; the Bill would add the National Song, Vande Mataram. The Bill has been introduced but not enacted.

## 2. Background
The Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971 protects the Constitution, the National Flag, and the National Anthem against specified insults. Its current section 3 punishes intentional prevention of anthem singing or disturbance of an assembly engaged in it. Vande Mataram was composed in Sanskritised Bengali by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and appeared in his novel Anandamath. On 24 January 1950, Constituent Assembly President Rajendra Prasad stated that Vande Mataram, which played a historic part in the freedom struggle, should be honoured equally with Jana Gana Mana, though the Assembly did not insert a separate constitutional provision declaring a national song.

## 3. Timeline
- **1870s–1882**: Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay composed Vande Mataram and included it in Anandamath, published in 1882.
- **1896**: Vande Mataram was sung at a session of the Indian National Congress.
- **24 January 1950**: Rajendra Prasad’s Constituent Assembly statement recognised Jana Gana Mana as the National Anthem and accorded Vande Mataram equal honour for its historic role.
- **23 December 1971**: Parliament enacted the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act.
- **24 July 2026**: The amendment Bill was introduced in the Rajya Sabha to extend section 3 to the National Song.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay**: Author and composer associated with Vande Mataram and Anandamath.
- **Rajendra Prasad**: President of the Constituent Assembly who made the 24 January 1950 statement on the anthem and song.
- **Parliament of India**: Enacted the 1971 Act and is considering the 2026 amendment.
- **Rajya Sabha**: House in which the 2026 Bill was introduced.
- **Ministry of Home Affairs**: Administrative ministry associated with the national-honour statute and national-symbol protocol.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
The Bill illustrates that the legal protection of national symbols is statutory and must be reconciled with constitutional freedoms. It criminalises intentional prevention or disturbance, not every failure to sing.

- **Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution**: protects freedom of speech and expression, subject to reasonable restrictions under Article 19(2).
- **Article 25 of the Constitution**: protects freedom of conscience and religion, subject to public order, morality, health, and other constitutional limits.
- **Article 51A(a) of the Constitution**: states the fundamental duty to abide by the Constitution and respect the National Flag and National Anthem.
- **Section 3 of the 1971 Act**: currently punishes intentional prevention of anthem singing or disturbance of an assembly, with imprisonment up to three years, fine, or both.
- **Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala (1986)**: protected students who respectfully stood but did not sing the National Anthem because of genuinely held religious belief; respectful silence was distinguished from intentional prevention or disturbance.

## 6. Static GK Connection
The national anthem and national song differ in authorship, language, history, and legal treatment.

- **Jana Gana Mana**: written by Rabindranath Tagore and adopted as India’s National Anthem on 24 January 1950.
- **Vande Mataram**: composed by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and historically associated with the freedom movement.
- **Anandamath**: Bankim’s 1882 novel containing Vande Mataram.
- **Constituent Assembly**: adopted the Constitution and was the forum for Rajendra Prasad’s statement on both songs.
- **Fundamental duties**: appear in Part IVA of the Constitution; Article 51A(a) expressly names the Flag and Anthem, not the National Song.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The 2026 amendment Bill introduced in the Rajya Sabha proposes to replace section 3 of the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971. The current section punishes a person who intentionally prevents the singing of the National Anthem or causes disturbance to an assembly engaged in such singing. The introduced text would extend the same rule to the National Song. The maximum punishment stated in the introduced Bill is three years’ imprisonment, a fine, or both. This wording focuses on intentional prevention and disturbance rather than imposing an unqualified duty on every person to sing. That distinction matters because the Supreme Court in Bijoe Emmanuel protected students who stood respectfully during the anthem but did not sing owing to sincere religious belief. Vande Mataram has historic national status through the freedom movement and Rajendra Prasad’s 24 January 1950 statement, while Article 51A(a) expressly refers to respecting the National Flag and National Anthem. As introduced, the Bill is a legislative proposal and not yet an enacted amendment.
