# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
On 1 August 2026, the Union government announced Cabinet approval for the National Offshore Exploration Scheme, called “Samudra Manthan”, with an outlay of ₹84,084 crore. The scheme is intended to reduce the financial and geological risks associated with searching for oil and natural gas in deepwater and ultra-deepwater areas. It turns an earlier policy proposal into an approved government programme and forms part of India’s attempt to improve domestic hydrocarbon discovery and reduce exposure to imported-energy shocks.

## 2. Background
India imports most of the crude oil it consumes, so disruptions at maritime chokepoints and changes in global prices affect inflation, the current account, public finances, and household energy costs. Offshore exploration is expensive because seismic surveys do not guarantee a commercial discovery and a single deepwater well can cost roughly ₹1,000–1,200 crore. Earlier licensing regimes opened exploration to public, private, and foreign firms, but high risk and modest discovery rates limited investment. Before Cabinet approval, reports said the proposed Samudra Manthan framework could reimburse part of exploratory drilling costs, cap support per well, assist 3D seismic surveys, and complement auctions of deepwater blocks. The approved scheme’s legally controlling terms are those notified by the government, not earlier media descriptions of the proposal.

## 3. Timeline
- **1997–1999**: The government approved and operationalised the New Exploration Licensing Policy (NELP) to attract risk capital and technology into oil and gas exploration.
- **10 March 2016**: The Cabinet approved the Hydrocarbon Exploration and Licensing Policy (HELP), later implemented through the Open Acreage Licensing Programme (OALP).
- **15 August 2025**: The Prime Minister announced a National Deep Water Exploration Mission under the “Samudra Manthan” vision.
- **28 July 2026**: Independent reports described a proposed deepwater-risk-sharing package and eighteen deepwater or ultra-deepwater blocks under auction.
- **1 August 2026**: The Union government announced Cabinet approval of the ₹84,084 crore National Offshore Exploration Scheme, Samudra Manthan.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Union Cabinet**: Approved the National Offshore Exploration Scheme and its overall outlay.
- **Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas**: Responsible for Union policy concerning petroleum, natural gas, exploration, and production.
- **Directorate General of Hydrocarbons**: Technical upstream regulator and custodian of exploration-policy and National Data Repository functions.
- **ONGC and Oil India**: National oil companies that undertake domestic onshore and offshore exploration.
- **Public, private, and foreign explorers**: Potential participants in licensed blocks and risk-sharing support, subject to the notified scheme and applicable contracts.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
Offshore petroleum is not governed merely by an executive slogan. Constitutional allocation, parliamentary legislation, licensing contracts, environmental requirements, and maritime jurisdiction operate together.

- **Article 297 of the Constitution**: vests things of value underlying the ocean within India’s territorial waters, continental shelf, and exclusive economic zone in the Union.
- **Union List Entry 53**: places regulation and development of oilfields, mineral oil resources, petroleum, and petroleum products within Parliament’s legislative field.
- **Oilfields (Regulation and Development) Act, 1948**: supplies the central statutory framework for regulation and development of oilfields and mineral-oil resources.
- **Maritime Zones Act, 1976**: defines India’s territorial sea, continental shelf, exclusive economic zone, and related Union rights.
- **Administrative approval versus notified terms**: Cabinet approval authorises the programme, but eligibility, reimbursement, safeguards, and implementation must be read from the final scheme documents and contracts.

## 6. Static GK Connection
The issue links energy economics with physical and political geography.

- **Deepwater and ultra-deepwater**: describe offshore environments where water depth, pressure, weather, logistics, and drilling complexity raise costs and risk.
- **HELP**: the Hydrocarbon Exploration and Licensing Policy uses a revenue-sharing model and a single licence for conventional and unconventional hydrocarbons.
- **OALP**: the Open Acreage Licensing Programme allows companies to express interest in exploration areas rather than waiting only for government-selected blocks.
- **National Data Repository**: provides geological and geophysical data intended to reduce information gaps for explorers.
- **Energy security**: means reliable, affordable, and diversified energy supply; it is broader than self-sufficiency and includes imports, reserves, transport routes, efficiency, and alternative energy.

## 7. CLAT Passage
India imports most of the crude oil it consumes, leaving its economy exposed to price increases and disruptions along maritime supply routes. Offshore basins may contain domestic oil and gas, but exploration is uncertain and costly: seismic data can identify prospects without proving that a commercially recoverable discovery exists, and a deepwater exploratory well can cost around ₹1,000–1,200 crore. On 1 August 2026, the Union government announced Cabinet approval of the ₹84,084 crore National Offshore Exploration Scheme, known as Samudra Manthan. Earlier reporting on the proposal described government risk-sharing for exploratory drilling and seismic surveys, alongside the auction of deepwater blocks. The final legal and financial obligations, however, must come from the notified scheme and exploration contracts. Constitutionally, Article 297 vests valuable resources beneath India’s territorial waters, continental shelf, and exclusive economic zone in the Union. Parliament’s Union List also assigns regulation and development of oilfields and mineral oil resources to the Union field. Samudra Manthan therefore combines economic policy, maritime geography, statutory licensing, public expenditure, and environmental oversight.

### Questions
1. **Which inference is best supported by the passage?**:
   - (A) Cabinet approval by itself proves that every exploratory well will discover commercially recoverable oil.
   - (B) Public risk-sharing may encourage exploration but cannot eliminate geological uncertainty.
   - (C) Article 297 transfers offshore petroleum ownership to private explorers.
   - (D) Seismic surveys are legally equivalent to a commercial discovery.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: The passage distinguishes financial risk-sharing from the continuing geological uncertainty of exploration.
2. **Why does the passage say that final scheme documents and contracts remain important after Cabinet approval?**:
   - (A) They determine operational eligibility, safeguards, and binding financial obligations.
   - (B) They can override the Constitution without legislation.
   - (C) They eliminate the need for environmental approvals.
   - (D) They automatically convert every offshore block into Union territory.
   - *Correct Answer*: A
   - *Explanation*: Cabinet approval establishes the programme, while notified terms and contracts specify how it operates.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Frequently confused with**: Samudra Manthan National Offshore Exploration Scheme versus the Deep Ocean Mission and Samudrayaan.
- **Why they differ**: Samudra Manthan is presented as an offshore oil-and-gas exploration and energy-security programme; the Deep Ocean Mission is an earth-science programme covering deep-sea technology, minerals, biodiversity, and ocean services.
- **Memory clue**: “Hydrocarbons use HELP; ocean science goes deep.”
