# Issue Dossier

## 1. What Happened
The Supreme Court concluded **Samadhan Samaroh 2026** with a three-day Special Lok Adalat held on 21, 22 and 23 August 2026. The name expands to **Supreme Court Action for Mediated Adjudication and Disputes Harmonization Across Nation**. The initiative had begun on 21 April with pre-settlement work through legal-services institutions and mediation centres; its stated purpose was amicable resolution of suitable matters pending before the Supreme Court through consent-based processes.

The Supreme Court's consolidated statement, released on 24 August, reported **3,285 matters listed**, **1,664 matters settled or disposed of through Lok Adalat**, and **48 matters resolved through mediation**—a total of **1,712 resolved matters**. It also reported disbursements of **₹2,40,94,37,087.38** (about ₹240.94 crore). The day-wise Lok Adalat figures were 402 disposals out of 588 listed on 21 August, 350 out of 1,159 on 22 August, and 912 out of 1,538 on 23 August. These consolidated figures supersede the interim counts reported while the exercise was still under way.

## 2. Background
Article 39A directs the State to promote justice on the basis of equal opportunity and provide free legal aid so that economic or other disabilities do not deny access to justice. Parliament enacted the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** to establish legal-services institutions and organise Lok Adalats. The Act creates a network that includes the National Legal Services Authority, State and District Authorities, High Court Legal Services Committees and the Supreme Court Legal Services Committee.

A Lok Adalat is a statutory alternative dispute-resolution forum for pending or pre-litigation disputes that can be settled. It helps parties reach an agreed solution; it does not conduct a conventional contested trial in a regular Lok Adalat. Samadhan Samaroh used nationwide pre-settlement coordination and Supreme Court Special Lok Adalat benches to address suitable matters already pending before the apex court.

## 4. Key People & Organisations
- **Supreme Court of India**: Organised the nationwide initiative and the culminating Special Lok Adalat.
- **Chief Justice of India Surya Kant**: Led the Court during the exercise.
- **Supreme Court Legal Services Committee**: A statutory committee that may organise Lok Adalats for Supreme Court matters.
- **National Legal Services Authority (NALSA)**: Coordinates legal-services policy and the national institutional network.
- **State, District, High Court and Taluk Legal Services institutions**: Conducted pre-settlement outreach and supported remote participation.
- **Special Lok Adalat benches**: Combined judges and participating advocates to facilitate consensual resolution.
- **Litigants and counsel**: Retained control over whether to settle; consent is central to a regular Lok Adalat award.

## 5. Legal & Constitutional Significance
- **Article 39A** supplies the constitutional directive for equal justice and free legal aid. It is a Directive Principle, not an automatic individual decree disposing of a case.
- **Sections 19 and 20 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** govern organisation of Lok Adalats and cognisance of suitable cases.
- **Section 21** provides that a Lok Adalat award is deemed to be a decree of a civil court or an order of another court, is final and binding on the parties, and is not appealable under the ordinary statutory route.
- When a pending matter is settled, the court fee is refundable in the manner prescribed by law.
- A **regular Lok Adalat** is conciliatory and depends on settlement. If no settlement is reached, the matter returns to the appropriate court; it cannot decide the dispute on merits merely because conciliation failed.
- A **Permanent Lok Adalat** under Chapter VI-A concerns specified public utility services and may, subject to statutory limits, decide a non-offence dispute on merits after conciliation fails. Samadhan Samaroh was not a Permanent Lok Adalat.
- A Lok Adalat award is binding because the statute attaches legal effect to a voluntary settlement, not because the forum exercises the Supreme Court's constitutional appellate jurisdiction in the ordinary manner.
- Consensual disposal can reduce pendency and cost, but it complements rather than replaces adjudication, appointments, case management and other structural reforms.

## 7. CLAT Passage
The Supreme Court's Samadhan Samaroh culminated in a three-day Special Lok Adalat after months of pre-settlement work. Suitable pending disputes were placed before benches that facilitated dialogue rather than conducting ordinary contested trials. Under the Legal Services Authorities Act, a regular Lok Adalat depends on the parties reaching a settlement. Once an award records that settlement, Section 21 treats it as a civil-court decree or an order of the relevant court, makes it final and binding, and bars an ordinary appeal. If settlement fails, however, a regular Lok Adalat cannot impose its preferred merits decision; the dispute returns to the appropriate court. This differs from a Permanent Lok Adalat for public utility services, which has a separate statutory scheme. The initiative may reduce delay and expense, but it does not eliminate the need for courts to adjudicate disputes that parties cannot lawfully or voluntarily settle.

1. **Why is the award of a regular Lok Adalat binding on the parties?**:
   - (A) Because every conciliator exercises constituent power.
   - (B) Because Section 21 gives legal effect to the parties' settlement.
   - (C) Because Article 39A abolishes all appeals.
   - (D) Because a Lok Adalat is a Permanent Lok Adalat in every case.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: The statute deems the settlement award a decree or order and makes it final and binding.
2. **What follows if parties before a regular Lok Adalat do not settle?**:
   - (A) The forum may invent a compromise and impose it.
   - (B) The dispute ordinarily returns to the appropriate court.
   - (C) The claim is automatically dismissed with prejudice.
   - (D) The Permanent Lok Adalat must convict one party.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: A regular Lok Adalat is settlement-based and does not decide the merits when conciliation fails.
3. **Which inference about Samadhan Samaroh is best supported by the passage?**:
   - (A) It replaced the Supreme Court's adjudicatory jurisdiction.
   - (B) It used consensual resolution as one tool for reducing pendency and cost.
   - (C) It converted every civil dispute into a public utility dispute.
   - (D) It made Article 39A a criminal offence.
   - *Correct Answer*: B
   - *Explanation*: The initiative complements adjudication by resolving suitable cases through voluntary settlement.

## 12. Confusion Traps
- **Lok Adalat vs court trial**: A regular Lok Adalat facilitates settlement; it does not decide contested merits after failure.
- **Regular vs Permanent Lok Adalat**: The latter has a special public-utility jurisdiction under Chapter VI-A.
- **Mediation vs Lok Adalat award**: Mediation facilitates negotiation; statutory finality attaches when a Lok Adalat records an award based on settlement.
- **Final award vs no judicial review at all**: No ordinary appeal lies, but constitutional courts may examine narrow jurisdictional or fraud-related challenges in an appropriate proceeding.
- **Special Lok Adalat vs special constitutional court**: “Special” describes the settlement exercise, not a new constitutional court.
- **Interim vs consolidated counts**: The first-day and Tripura reports were partial snapshots; use the Supreme Court's 24 August consolidated total of 1,712 resolved matters.
